# The gap: our Plexus cilium vs the real Platynereis swim (2026-09-23)

## Big picture first: how an oscillator in water propels at all

At this scale water is all viscosity, no inertia (Reynolds ~1e-4), so flow is time-reversible: a
plain back-and-forth wiggle pulls back exactly what it pushed and goes NOWHERE (Purcell's scallop
theorem). To swim, the oscillator must break time-symmetry -- make the forward and return strokes
not cancel. A cilium does it by DRAG ANISOTROPY: a thin filament drags ~2x harder moving broadside
than edge-on (zeta_perp/zeta_par ~ 2, Gray & Hancock), so it pushes hard broadside (power stroke)
and slips back edge-on (recovery) -- same wiggle, unequal water moved, net thrust. The water goes
one way, the force-free cell recoils the other. That is the entire engine, and it is the `ratio`
in our drag: ratio 1 (isotropic) is a reciprocal paddle that cannot swim (our control); ratio 2
swims. The open question is whether we truly broke the symmetry or the cell just jiggles -- the
10 s run decides (straight-line distance = swim; wandering ~1 um = jiggle).

## What we built (Plexus), coarse -> specific

A cilium is a DISCRETE ELASTIC ROD (a chain of nodes, thickness = a drag coefficient, no grid
floor) coupled to a MATERIAL-POINT-METHOD (MPM) water. Operators (each a set->delta; the engine
integrates):
- `rod_seed` lays the nodes + the segment table.
- `rod_elastic`: stretch `f = k_s(|e|-rest) e_hat`; bend `g = k_b * c`, `c` the discrete curvature
  (graph Laplacian), internal by construction.
- `rod_base`: pin `a0 = w_n^2 (target - x0) - 2 z w_n (v0 - vt)`; a vector direction clamp
  `tau = -(clamp^2) th k_hat - 2 z_c clamp w`; reaction on the anchoring cell.
- `rod_motor`: the drive, distributed. Prescribed wave `M_i = k_m h^3 A sin(w t - 2pi s_i/lam)`,
  applied as grad of `E = sum M_i th_i` about the parallel-transported beat normal (force- and
  torque-free). OR Brokaw curvature-control `M_i = m_sat tanh(gain * sum_{k>i} th_k)`.
- `drag` (overdamped): `v_rod = u + f_int/zeta`; hands the fluid `f = -zeta*coupling*(dv - (1-1/ratio) dv_par)`,
  Gray-Hancock anisotropy `ratio = zeta_perp/zeta_par ~ 2`. Momentum-conserving.
Working point: 8 nodes, `k_bend = B/(zeta_perp h^4) = 4150/s`, dt 1e-5, coupling 0.1. Result:
5 cilia on a 2 um sphere, prescribed 10 Hz beat, anisotropic drag -> cell moves ~1 um in 0.5 s.

## The target (Poon 2025)

150 um larva, prototroch = 12 multiciliated cells x 500-700 cilia (~20 um long, 0.2 um wide,
0.57 um apart), beat 10 Hz, DEXIOPLECTIC metachronal wave of wavelength ~L/2, swims a left-handed
helix. Coupling is STERIC (contact) within a cell, NOT hydrodynamic; global flow has no effect;
neural (cholinergic) arrest + (serotonergic) frequency control steer it.

## THE GAP, ranked, each tied to a paper we read

1. **Wrong fluid regime.** Cilia swim at Re ~ 1e-4 (Lauga & Powers 2009: bacterium 1e-5..1e-4,
   sperm 1e-2). Our MPM larva ran at Re ~ 1.5e4 -- 6-8 orders too high. MPM carries inertia and
   is compressible, so it breaks time-reversibility and the 1/r Stokeslet decay the real physics
   needs. This is why `coupling: 1.0` diverges and 0.1 is a grid patch, and why our absolute
   speeds are meaningless. **The single biggest gap.** The right analog (Rode 2021) uses a LOW-Re
   mesoscale fluid (MPC), not MPM.
2. **The metachronal coupling is STERIC, and we test hydrodynamic.** Poon 2025's central result:
   ablation shows global flow does nothing; coordination is short-range contact at 0.57 um. Our
   P3 tried to earn synchrony through the shared MPM fluid and got r flat -- which is now CONFIRMED
   as the right answer to the WRONG-tested mechanism. To get metachrony we need a steric
   cilium-cilium contact law at ~0.57 um, which our 1.56 um grid cannot resolve and no operator
   provides. And the wave is dexioplectic lambda~L/2, which pure hydrodynamics cannot make
   (gives antiplectic/symplectic, lambda >= 2L).
3. **Scale and geometry.** Rode 2021: swim speed ~ N^0.7, studied 5 < N < 50 cilia with cilium
   length = 3x body radius. We have N=5 on a body 10x SMALLER than the cilium (2 um sphere, 20 um
   cilium) -- the wrong aspect ratio and the bottom of the N range. The real cell is 40 um with
   500-700 cilia; the band is 12 such cells on a 150 um sphere. We are far below the propulsion
   regime, and a symmetric handful on one sphere cannot make the helix (Rode: chirality of the
   wave makes the helix).
4. **The beat is prescribed, not emergent.** Sartori 2016: the real beat is curvature feedback
   with motors responding to the RATE of curvature; it needs ~20 tracked points. Our Brokaw
   curvature-control is one rung below and cannot host a wave at 8 nodes (dt ~ nodes^-4 wall).
   Prescribed wave is a stated reduction.
5. **No neural control.** Verciszto 2017 / Poon 2025: the larva steers by ciliary ARREST
   (cholinergic) and frequency change (serotonergic), not by muscle. We have none.
6. **Thrust anisotropy is an explicit patch.** We ADDED Gray-Hancock `ratio` because the MPM
   fluid does not resolve the cilium; in a resolved low-Re fluid it would emerge. So P4's swim
   uses the right mechanism (Gray & Hancock 1955) implemented as a reduction.

## The honest doubt (Cedric): swim or random walk?

The ~1 um over 0.5 s is NOT clearly directed -- net drift (~0.4 um) ~ beat wobble (~0.8 um).
Directed swimming grows LINEAR in t; a biased random walk / transient grows as sqrt(t) and its net
displacement wanders. Test running: `p6_swim10s`, 20 Hz / ratio 2, 10 s = 200 beats. If z(t) is
linear to ~14 um it swims; if it stays ~1-2 um the P4/P5 "swim" was wobble and gaps 1-3 above are
why. Prediction to confront honestly either way.


## MEASURED Reynolds number (2026-09-23, run nu_cilium, shear wave at the cilium's own grid/dt)

`tools/nu_effective.py` fits the decay of a shear wave u = U0 sin(kz) as exp(-nu_eff k^2 t).
At the cilium's settings (grid 32, dt 1e-5, cilium water) the measured EFFECTIVE kinematic
viscosity is nu_eff = 0.449 (r^2 0.89) -- and it is NUMERICAL (grid/dt), not the spec's eta
(nu runs with 1000x eta gave the same nu_eff). Re = U L / nu_eff with U ~ 5 world/s (tip 250 um/s),
L = 0.4 world (cilium length 20 um) -> Re = 4.5; L = 0.004 (diameter 0.2 um) -> Re = 0.045.
Real cilium: Re ~ 5e-3 (length) / 5e-5 (diameter). So we run ~3 ORDERS TOO INERTIAL. On the beat
(length) scale Re ~ 4.5 > 1: NOT Stokes. nu_eff ~ 1/dt, so dt 1e-6 -> Re ~ 0.45, dt 1e-7 -> ~0.045;
reaching the real 5e-3 by dt alone is ~1000x (impractical). Retune = smaller dt and/or slower beat,
re-measured with nu_cilium each time. THIS IS THE FIRST-PLACE FIX: until Re << 1 the swim and
coupling numbers are in the wrong regime.


## Reynolds-reduction campaign result (2026-09-23, SI units, runs re_s3..re_s7 + re_target/seawater)

Aim: nu_eff = 1.0e-6 m^2/s (real seawater) -> Re = U L / nu_eff = 5e-3 (U 2.5e-4 m/s tip, L 2.0e-5 m,
both FIXED by biology; only nu_eff tuned). Knob: fluid viscosity (mpm_viscosity eta) + timestep.
Each step archived (mp4 + 3 curves, axis limits from the data, tools/reynolds_curves.py).

RELIABLE result: Re driven 4.5 -> 0.26 (nu_eff 1.1e-9 -> 1.9e-8 m^2/s), fits r^2 > 0.98. Still ~50x
above real seawater. Below Re ~0.26 TWO WALLS:
1. MEASUREMENT: near seawater viscosity the shear wave decays within a few frames -> fits collapse
   (r^2 0.55, one dial-in gave NEGATIVE nu_eff), and nu_eff-vs-dt is NON-MONOTONIC (dt 2e-8->nu 100,
   1.3e-8->garbage, 1e-8->825). The low-Re "viscosity" is dt-dependent NUMERICAL dissipation, not a
   controllable physical viscosity -- the physical eta is attenuated ~12-30x and saturates.
2. FEASIBILITY: reaching Re 0.26 already needed dt 1e-7 (100x the cilium's 1e-5); real seawater needs
   dt ~1e-8, at which a COUPLED beating cilium (0.5 s = 5e7 frames) is ~weeks per run.

CONCLUSION: MPM can nudge the fluid toward low Re but CANNOT host a coupled low-Reynolds cilium at
feasible cost or with clean viscosity control. This matches the code audit: the field uses Stokes /
regularized-Stokeslet solvers (Re = 0 by construction), not MPM, for cilia/flagella. The honest fix
is a Stokes/RFT fluid coupling, not tuning MPM. Reusable references cloned in papers/code
(PyStokes, IB2d) and named (icemtel/carpet, PyElastica, NEAREST).


## CORRECTION 2026-09-23 (fig 0530): the fluid DOES reach seawater -- earlier low-Re points were mismeasured

Cedric's method checks (bigger seed amplitude, denser sampling, "show me the fit") exposed a BUG in
the measurement, not a physics wall. `nu_effective` fits the middle 15-75% of the run; for a fast
(high-viscosity) decay the wave is gone within the first ~0.08 ms and that window sits in the flat
NOISE FLOOR, so it fitted noise and returned negative nu_eff (the "non-monotonic / unreliable" mess).
Re-run with amplitude x40 and dense sampling (re_clean, dt 2e-8, eta 1e7), fitting the EARLY clean
decay: nu_eff = 5.0e-7 m^2/s, Re = 0.010, r^2 = 0.94 -- essentially real seawater (1e-6 m^2/s, Re 5e-3).
So the FLUID reaches the low-Re regime and it is cleanly measurable with the right fit window. The
open wall is unchanged and is FEASIBILITY, not regime: seawater viscosity needs dt ~2e-8, at which a
COUPLED beating cilium (0.5 s = 2.5e7 frames) is ~days per run. TODO: fix nu_effective to auto-detect
the early-decay window; re-measure the ladder for corrected Re numbers.
