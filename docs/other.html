Other models

The same decomposition, on models that are not biological tissue. Each family below is taken apart into the operator kinds of the main page — a stigmergic colony over a set and a field, a chaotic flow as one operator, agent interaction as a rewire and its laterals, and gravity as a single lateral summed over every pair. Hover (or tab to) a title to read the exact spec that generated it.

Stigmergy — Physarum slime (one species, a disc colony)

Stigmergy has been decomposed into five operators over one set and one field: the agents deposit a trail into the field and sense it back (exchange), the field diffuses and decays under its own dynamics (field), and a move carries each agent along its heading. By composing these with different turn rates and sensor geometries we can build different transport networks out of the same five. Shown here is a single species seeded in a disc, so the colony stays a bounded circular web.

Reference — the model is Jones (2010), Artificial Life 16(2):127–153, whose sense-and-deposit agent this is; the implementation we adapted is Sebastian Lague’s Slime-Simulation, and the name for the mechanism is Grassé’s (1959) stigmergy.

Stigmergy — Physarum slime (many species, space-filling)

The same five operators, with the agents seeded across the whole field instead of in a disc and split into several species, each depositing and sensing its own trail channel while inhibiting the others. By composing them with different numbers of species we fill the plane instead of bounding a colony. Shown here are four, six and eight species, segregating into interwoven networks.

Reference — the model is Jones (2010), Artificial Life 16(2):127–153, whose sense-and-deposit agent this is; the implementation we adapted is Sebastian Lague’s Slime-Simulation, and the name for the mechanism is Grassé’s (1959) stigmergy.

Strange attractors

A dissipative chaotic flow has been decomposed into a single operator: it carries every point of a set along ẋ = f(x), with no relation between the points at all. By composing that one operator with different flows named in the spec we get every attractor in the family — ten of them ship. Shown here are six, each a seed cube stretched and folded until the cloud is the attractor itself.

Reference — Lorenz (1963), J. Atmos. Sci. 20:130–141; Thomas (1999), Int. J. Bifurc. Chaos 9:1889–1905; Sprott (1994), Phys. Rev. E 50:R647–R650; Dadras & Momeni (2009), Phys. Lett. A 373:3637–3642. The Halvorsen and Aizawa systems have no primary paper we can point at — they are catalogued rather than published, Halvorsen in Sprott’s Chaos and Time-Series Analysis (2003) — and are named here as folklore rather than cited as literature.

Interaction & flocking

Agent interaction has been decomposed into a rewire that rebuilds a radius neighbour graph from the current positions, and lateral operators that act along it. By composing different laterals on that one graph we can build different collective behaviours: a per-type force law, or the three boids rules, or alignment with self-propulsion. Shown here are particles sorting themselves by type, a flock, and an ordered swarm.

Reference — boids is Reynolds (1987), SIGGRAPH Comput. Graph. 21(4):25–34, the soft-core pair potential is D’Orsogna et al. (2006), Phys. Rev. Lett. 96:104302, and the alignment model is Vicsek et al. (1995), Phys. Rev. Lett. 75:1226–1229; the implementations are adapted from ParticleGraph (Allier et al.).

Inverse-square law — self-gravity

Gravity has been decomposed into a single lateral operator summed over every pair of a set: mass, always attracting. By composing it over sets of different composition we can build different systems without ever writing a second law — two galaxies placed in one set pull on each other through the same operator that condensed each of them. Shown here are a disc condensing in 2D and in 3D, a heavier central mass, four clumps merging, and two galaxies meeting on a grazing and on a head-on course.

Reference — softened self-gravity after Philip Mocz’s Create Your Own N-body Simulation (2020); the encounter geometry after Toomre & Toomre, Galactic Bridges and Tails (1972).
