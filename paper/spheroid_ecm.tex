\section*{1.\; Sets, operators, schedule}

A Plexus model is sets $\times$ fields $\times$ operators $\times$ schedule. The biological hierarchy
here is four entities and one field; the basement membrane is a \emph{specialised} ECM, not the ECM:

\smallskip
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
entity & set & representation & principal operators\\
\midrule
epithelium & \op{vertex}, \op{cell} & 3D active vertex model & \op{shape\_energy\_3d}, \op{morphogen\_growth\_3d}, \op{divide\_3d}\\
junction & relation on the mesh & half-edges, keyed state & \op{junction\_myosin}, \op{reconnect\_t1\_3d}\\
basement membrane & \op{basement\_membrane\_particle} & MPM + explicit bonds & \op{..\_seed/bond/remodel/bond\_break}, \op{integrin\_adhesion}\\
interstitial ECM & \op{mpm\_particle} & MPM fibres & \op{ecm\_seed}, \op{ecm\_stress}, \op{cell\_to\_ecm}\\
(field) & \op{mpm\_grid} & MLS-MPM background grid & \op{mpm\_scatter/gather/grid\_update}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\smallskip

Every particle body scatters into and gathers from the \emph{same} grid; that sharing \emph{is} the
mechanical coupling between them, so no pairwise contact model is written. Operators carry one of seven
kinds. The four dynamics kinds return a per-set delta the engine integrates once per tick; \op{field},
\op{rewire} and \op{structural} mutate the grid, the relation $E$, or the membership $|S|$ in place and
\emph{return nothing}. That last fact drives a design decision in \S5.

\section*{2.\; The spheroid: 3D active vertex model}

Cells are faces of a closed triangulated shell; cell $f$'s volume is the wedge from the tissue centre,
$v_f=\tfrac13\,\mathbf{c}_f\!\cdot\!\mathbf{N}_f$. The energy is
%
\begin{equation}
E \;=\; \sum_f\Bigl[K_A\,(A_f-A_f^0)^2 + K_P\,(P_f-P_f^0)^2 + \tfrac12\Gamma P_f^2 + K_V\,(v_f-v_f^0)^2\Bigr]
\;+\;\Lambda\!\sum_e m_e\,\ell_e\;+\;K_R\sum_i\bigl(|\mathbf{x}_i|-R_0\bigr)^2 ,
\label{eq:avm}
\end{equation}
%
relaxed overdamped each frame, $\mathbf{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}-\eta\mu\,\nabla E$ with a per-step cap.
The factor $m_e$ is new (\S5); with $m_e\equiv1$ Eq.~\eqref{eq:avm} is the stock Okuda energy and every
prior run is bit-identical. Growth scales each cell's targets by a cumulative $s_f$,
%
\begin{equation}
s_f \leftarrow s_f\bigl(1+\lambda(\rho+\mathrm{Hill}(a_f))\bigr),\qquad
A_f^0=A_f^{0,\text{init}}s_f^2,\quad P_f^0=P_f^{0,\text{init}}s_f,\quad v_f^0=v_f^{0,\text{init}}s_f^3,
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
%
and \op{divide\_3d} splits a cell once $v_f$ passes twice its reference. From $200$ cells the tissue
reaches $\sim6000$ and its apical radius $4.66\to16.6$ (arbitrary units) in $402$ frames.

\section*{3.\; The ECM: MLS-MPM}

The matrix is material points on a background grid, fixed-corotated with $F=RS$:
%
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\tau} \;=\; 2\mu\,(F-R)F^{\!\top} + \lambda J (J-1)\,I ,
\qquad \boldsymbol{\sigma} \;=\; \boldsymbol{\tau}/J ,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{vM}}=\sqrt{\tfrac32\,\mathbf{s}\!:\!\mathbf{s}},\;\;
\mathbf{s}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\tfrac13\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})I .
\label{eq:mpm}
\end{equation}
%
$\boldsymbol{\tau}$ was already computed every substep to build the affine momentum matrix and then
discarded; \op{mpm\_scatter} now optionally caches the Cauchy stress $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ to a
per-particle buffer (\texttt{store\_stress}, off by default, byte-identical when off). This matters
quantitatively: colouring by the volumetric measure $|J-1|$ put $0.6\%$ of the matrix above the display
floor, whereas $\sigma_{\mathrm{vM}}$ of the \emph{same} run puts $64\%$ there. A matrix pushed by a
growing sphere \emph{shears}; the fixed-corotated law resists volume change far more stiffly than shape
change, so the volumetric measure was blind to the deformation, not the mechanics.

\section*{4.\; Spheroid $\leftrightarrow$ ECM}

The two solvers cannot share a world. \op{mpm\_grid} is hard-coded to $[0,1]^3$; the epithelium lives in
a 50-unit box, and rescaling it there was tried and measured, not assumed --- the same run gives
$200\!\to\!319$ cells at its own scale but $200\!\to\!212$ rescaled, because the terms of the vertex
energy carry different powers of length and a $50\times$ shrink changes which one wins. So the two run as
two passes joined by one geometric scale, applied to a \emph{recorded} shape.

That recording is the whole coupling, and it runs one way. The epithelium's surface is a moving boundary
the matrix feels; nothing the matrix does reaches back. Fibres inside the surface are pushed out by a
soft penalty and then, as a backstop, projected out:
%
\begin{equation}\mathbf a_i \;=\; k_c\,\bigl(R(\hat{\mathbf u}_i,t)-r_i\bigr)_+\,\hat{\mathbf u}_i,
\label{eq:contact}\end{equation}
%
and the reaction is binned by direction and divided by the area each bin covers, $R^2\,d\Omega$, to give
a pressure map $P(\theta,\phi,t)$. A cell facing a stressed matrix then grows more slowly:
%
\begin{equation}g \;=\; f + \frac{1-f}{1+\bigl(P/p_{1/2}\bigr)^{n}},\qquad
 s_{\text{now}} \leftarrow s_{\text{prev}}\,\bigl(1+g\,(f_{\text{grow}}-1)\bigr).
\label{eq:gate}\end{equation}
%
Because it is the growth \emph{rate} that is gated, a small difference compounds over 400 frames, and a
$2.75\times$ stress anisotropy becomes a shape: an oblate cavity takes the tissue to oblateness $1.43$
where a flat pattern of the same strength leaves it round at $1.015$.

\section*{5.\; The two new levels}

\paragraph{Junctions.} In the vertex model contractility is one number for the whole tissue. Each
junction now carries its own myosin $m_e$, multiplying the line tension, and chasing a setpoint on a
timescale $\tau_m$:
%
\begin{equation}\Lambda\sum_e \ell_e \;\longrightarrow\; \Lambda\sum_e m_e\,\ell_e,\qquad
 m^{ss}_e = a\Bigl(1+\beta\,\bigl(x_e/\langle x\rangle-1\bigr)\Bigr),\qquad
 \dot m_e=\bigl(m^{ss}_e-m_e\bigr)/\tau_m.
\label{eq:myo}\end{equation}
%
What $x_e$ should be is the substance. Our first choice was junction \emph{length}, which makes the loop
\emph{longer $\to$ more myosin $\to$ shorter} --- a negative feedback that evens junction lengths out. The
measured biology is the opposite: myosin is recruited by \emph{tension} and is enriched on
\emph{disassembling} junctions, giving a positive feedback that drives neighbour exchange. Worse, in the
fast-myosin limit --- the correct one, since myosin turns over in seconds --- substituting the setpoint
turns Eq.~\eqref{eq:myo} into a line tension plus a harmonic spring on each edge, so ``$\beta$ reduces
the spread of junction lengths'' is that spring's definition and cannot be evidence for it. $x_e$ is now
the actual edge tension, $\Lambda m_e + 2K_P(P_f-P_f^0)+\Gamma P_f$, taken from the same energy the
mechanics minimises; and the observable we report is the \textbf{T1 rate}, which the two feedbacks move
in opposite directions where the length spread does not.

\paragraph{Basement membrane.} A sheet of nodes joined by explicit crosslinks --- MPM particles alone
have no connectivity, so they cannot be crosslinked and cannot fragment. Each crosslink is a spring that
forgets being stretched on a timescale $\tau_r$ and breaks past a strain $\varepsilon_b$; each node is
tied to a fixed \emph{direction} on the recorded surface, which is what makes it adhesion rather than
contact, since anchoring to the nearest point would let the node slide and the sheet would never stretch:
%
\begin{align}
\mathbf f_{ij}&=k_b\,(L_{ij}-\ell^0_{ij})\,\hat{\mathbf d}_{ij}, &
\dot\ell^0_{ij}&=(L_{ij}-\ell^0_{ij})/\tau_r, &
&\text{bond dies if } (L-\ell^0)/\ell^0>\varepsilon_b,
\label{eq:bond}\\[2pt]
\mathbf a_i&=\kappa\bigl(\mathbf c+\hat{\mathbf u}_i(R(\hat{\mathbf u}_i,t)+\delta)-\mathbf x_i\bigr), &
&\hat{\mathbf u}_i \text{ frozen at seeding.}
\label{eq:integrin}
\end{align}
%
Remodelling is not optional: a purely elastic sheet cannot enclose a surface that grows $260\%$ in linear
strain, and it tore at every $\varepsilon_b$ we tried. With it, the sheet settles at
$\langle\epsilon\rangle\simeq\dot\varepsilon\,\tau_r$. And because area grows as $R^2$ while deposition
adds a fixed fraction of existing material per frame, secretion has a threshold, $\rho^\ast=2\ln(R_1/R_0)/N$
--- though that follows from the operator's own setpoint, so measuring it confirms an implementation
rather than testing a hypothesis.

Two corrections. The sheet was integrated with a \emph{mass}, and everything this section used to report
about oscillation, critical damping, tracking lag and a stiffness ceiling followed from that; at
$\mathrm{Re}\sim10^{-10}$ the motion is $\gamma\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F$ and none of it exists. It is now
overdamped. And the metric these claims rested on --- the largest connected component --- was
\texttt{NaN} on every frame any analysis read, so it had never once been reported; it is now carried
forward beside the mean degree $z$, which must exceed $\approx\!4$ for the sheet to be mechanically a
sheet at all.

Finally, on naming: \op{integrin\_adhesion} is a tether to a surface, not a receptor. In mammals
$\alpha_1\beta_1$ and $\alpha_2\beta_1$ do bind collagen~IV, so the receptor exists --- but the dominant
epithelial anchor is to \emph{laminin}, which is not modelled, and the sheet has no attachment to the
stroma either. Removing the tether therefore leaves it attached to nothing, which is why that run is a
control and not a knockout.

\section*{6.\; The two schematics}

The two levels added on top of the spheroid and the matrix. Everything else about them --- the
configurations, the measurements, and the external audit that found much of it wanting --- has moved to
\texttt{spheroid\_ecm\_archive.tex} while the model is being corrected.
