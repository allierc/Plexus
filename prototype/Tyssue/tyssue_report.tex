\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This is the companion to the Turing--vertex prototype. There we modelled epithelial mechanics
as a \emph{Self-Propelled Voronoi} (SPV) tissue --- each cell a point, the polygon its Voronoi
cell, the mesh re-tessellated every tick so neighbour exchanges (T1) were implicit. That report
closed on a clear diagnosis: clean \emph{tubulation} was gated not on chemistry but on two
mechanics ingredients the Voronoi route structurally lacks --- (i) \emph{force-balance iteration}
and (ii) an \emph{explicit reconnection} (T1) operator. Here we build the sibling implementation:
a \emph{true vertex model} (AVM) after \texttt{tyssue}, whose degrees of freedom are the
\emph{vertices} of a half-edge mesh, in which those two ingredients are native. The same
epithelial shape-energy contract, a genuinely different numerical realisation --- the ``one
contract, many implementations'' thesis of \texttt{plexus2}~\S5, made concrete. This document
records the model-deciphering as it happens; findings are added as each stage lands.
\textbf{[Results sections are filled in incrementally --- see the dated field notes.]}
\end{abstract}

\section{Two implementations of one contract}
Both prototypes realise the same biological operator --- an epithelial \emph{mechanical
interaction} whose energy is a per-cell area/perimeter shape energy
\[
E \;=\; \sum_f \Big[ K_A (A_f - A_f^0)^2 + K_P (P_f - P_f^0)^2 \Big]
        \;+\; \tfrac12\Gamma \sum_f P_f^2 \;+\; \Lambda \sum_e \ell_e/2 ,
\]
with a target shape index $p_0 = P^0/\sqrt{A^0}$ tuning a rigidity transition at
$p_0^\ast\!\approx\!3.81$. They differ only in \emph{how} the polygons and their motion are
represented:

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.1cm}p{5.3cm}p{5.3cm}@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
degrees of freedom & cell \emph{centres} (points) & mesh \emph{vertices} (half-edge mesh) \\
polygon & Voronoi cell of the centres & explicit face = ring of shared vertices \\
adjacency / T1 & re-tessellate each tick $\Rightarrow$ \emph{implicit} & \emph{explicit} local edge collapse\,+\,split \\
force balance & overdamped Euler steps & gradient descent to residual (quasistatic) \\
cost & Delaunay per tick (scipy) & one scatter-add + one autograd pass \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The AVM is the missing sibling: it supplies force balance and explicit T1, which is exactly
what the SPV report identified as the tubulation bottleneck. Concretely, both are registered
\texttt{plexus2} operators acting on a \texttt{set}; the SPV acts on a \texttt{cell} set, the
AVM on a \texttt{vertex} set with the faces carried as a half-edge table.

\paragraph{The original (Okuda) is a 3D vertex model --- this is the 2D rung of that ladder.}
Okuda \emph{et al.}\ is a \emph{true 3D} vertex model: cells are polyhedra sharing polygonal
faces, the DOF are the junction vertices $\mathbf r_i$ (overdamped
$\eta_i(\dot{\mathbf r}_i-\mathbf V_{fi})=-\nabla_i U$), the energy
$U=\sum_j \tfrac12 k_v(v_j-v_j^{\mathrm{eq}})^2+\kappa_s s_j$ is over each cell's polyhedral
\emph{volume} and \emph{surface}, the balance is iterated to residual $<10^{-5}$ every
$\Delta t_v$, neighbour changes are explicit \emph{reversible network reconnection} (RNR / T1)
every $\Delta t_r$, and division inserts a new face. The prototype here is the \emph{2D} rung of
that same ladder --- vertices, force balance, explicit T1 --- with an area/perimeter energy
standing in for volume/surface. That is deliberate: 2D is where T1 is a clean edge flip, so we
validate force balance and reconnection there first. tyssue itself carries the 3D rungs
(\texttt{Monolayer}/\texttt{Bulk}: \texttt{cell\_volume} elasticity, the IH/HI 3D reconnections,
\texttt{extrude} apical$\to$3D), all catalogued in the extraction (\S4) --- so the path to Okuda's
elongated \emph{tubes} (rather than the SPV prototype's differential-growth \emph{lobes}) is to
lift this 2D core to a monolayer once the 2D topology operators are validated.

\section{The model in one paragraph}
The mechanical set is the \texttt{vertex} (state \texttt{pos}); the tissue topology is a
\emph{half-edge table} $(\mathrm{srce},\mathrm{trgt},\mathrm{face})$ ordered counter-clockwise
around each face, stashed on the vertex \texttt{Level} exactly as the SPV prototype stashed its
Voronoi rings. Per-face targets $A^0,P^0$ and liveness live alongside it. \texttt{mesh\_seed}
bootstraps a clean honeycomb by taking the Voronoi tessellation of a triangular lattice
\emph{once} (outer rings dropped, their shared vertices pinned $\to$ a fixed-border patch), then
the AVM takes over. \texttt{shape\_energy} computes every face's area and perimeter with a
single \texttt{index\_add} over the half-edge table, forms the shape energy, and takes the force
$-\nabla E$ on the vertices by one autograd pass; an inner loop of \texttt{relax\_iters} gradient
steps per tick returns the net displacement as a velocity, so each rendered frame is
(near-)force-balanced --- the quasistatic force balance the original does with \texttt{scipy}
L-BFGS, here vectorised and differentiable. Boundary (pinned) vertices are held fixed.

\paragraph{A half-edge primer.}
A \emph{half-edge} is a \emph{directed} edge $A\!\to\!B$ that belongs to exactly one face; the
real edge shared by cells $L$ and $R$ is stored as two opposite half-edges. Each is three
integers $(\mathrm{srce},\mathrm{trgt},\mathrm{face})$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{verbatim}
             cell L   (interior on the left)
     ... -> A --------------> B -> ...    half-edge  A->B ,  face = L
     ... <- A <-------------- B <- ...    half-edge  B->A ,  face = R
             cell R
   E_srce[k] = A     E_trgt[k] = B     E_face[k] = L   (one row per half-edge)
\end{verbatim}
\end{center}
A face is the ring of its half-edges in CCW order, so orientation --- and a positive shoelace
area --- is automatic; the two half-edges of a junction name its two neighbour cells, which is
exactly what a T1 needs (grab $A\!\to\!B$ in $L$ and $B\!\to\!A$ in $R$, collapse, re-split); and
per-face area/perimeter are a single \texttt{index\_add} keyed by \texttt{E\_face} (no per-cell
loop, so the mechanics vectorise). In \texttt{plexus2} terms \texttt{srce}/\texttt{trgt}/%
\texttt{face} are three index-buffer \emph{maps} on the set: \texttt{srce}/\texttt{trgt} are
edge$\to$vertex incidence, \texttt{face} is edge$\to$cell containment; gathering a cell value onto
its half-edges is a \emph{broadcast}, reducing half-edges to a cell is an \emph{aggregate}. Line
tension is $\Lambda\ell/2$ \emph{per half-edge}, so the two halves sum to $\Lambda\ell$ per real
edge --- tyssue's half-edge convention.

\section{Design decisions deciphered so far}
\emph{(These are the modelling choices that were not obvious from the paper and cost thought.)}

\paragraph{Vertices, not cells, are the set.}
The natural \texttt{plexus2} temptation is to keep the \texttt{cell} set of the SPV prototype and
bolt a mesh on. That is wrong: the AVM \emph{integrates the vertices}. Cells share vertices, so a
cell is not an independent particle --- it is a face, a derived readout over a ring of the vertex
set. The clean mapping is therefore a \texttt{vertex} set (the DOF) plus the half-edge table as
its topology map; faces never get their own integrated \texttt{Level}. Area/perimeter are
\emph{aggregate} readouts (vertices\,$\to$\,face), the force is their gradient back on the
vertices --- a genuine cross-scale (aggregate/broadcast) pair, richer than the single-set SPV.

\paragraph{Bootstrap the mesh from a Voronoi, then leave Voronoi behind.}
Hand-building a periodic honeycomb half-edge mesh with correct orientation is fiddly and
error-prone. Taking the Voronoi tessellation of a jittered triangular lattice \emph{once} gives a
valid honeycomb for free (scipy already de-duplicates the Voronoi vertices globally, so shared
vertices are shared indices). We keep only interior faces (finite Voronoi regions away from the
ragged border) and pin the boundary. After frame~0 the Voronoi is never used again --- the mesh
evolves by force balance and, later, explicit T1.

\paragraph{Force balance = inner relaxation loop, expressed as a velocity.}
A \texttt{plexus2} dynamics operator must \emph{return a delta}, not mutate positions. To emulate
tyssue's ``minimise to residual between topology events'' inside that contract, the operator runs
\texttt{relax\_iters} gradient-descent steps internally and returns
$v=(x_{\text{relaxed}}-x_0)/\Delta t$; the engine's $x \mathrel{+}= \Delta t\, v$ then lands on the
relaxed state. The $\Delta t$ cancels, so this is a pure per-frame relaxation and $\Delta t$ is a
free label --- the opposite of the SPV, where $\Delta t$ was the master knob that detuned the
mechanics. \emph{This alone answers the SPV report's ``force-balance iteration is missing''.}

\section{Stage 1: force-balanced relaxation and the rigidity transition}
A jittered honeycomb of $251$ interior cells ($566$ vertices, border pinned) is relaxed to
force balance by \texttt{shape\_energy} (\texttt{relax\_iters}$=20$ inner steps/frame,
$120$ frames), sweeping the target shape index $p_0\in[3.6,4.1]$. The residual (relaxed)
energy per cell and the relaxed mean shape index $\langle q\rangle = P/\sqrt{A}$ are the
order parameters of the rigidity transition (Bi \emph{et al.}, \emph{Nat.\ Phys.}\ 2015).

\section{Operator extraction: the tyssue atlas}
Beyond completing the tubulation story, tyssue is a rich enough simulator to be worth
\emph{decomposing in full} into the Plexus operator algebra (plexus2 App.~B \& H). We read every
subsystem --- \texttt{dynamics}, \texttt{topology}, \texttt{geometry}/\texttt{core},
\texttt{behaviors}/\texttt{solvers}/\texttt{collisions} --- and normalised each mechanism to a
typed contract. The full record is \texttt{tyssue\_atlas.yaml}; the structure and the saturation
result are below.

\paragraph{One structural picture.} tyssue is a \emph{single half-edge \texttt{Level}}: only
\texttt{vert.pos} is an integrated mechanical DOF; the \texttt{edge} set carries every incidence
map ($\mathrm{srce},\mathrm{trgt},\mathrm{face},\mathrm{cell},\mathrm{opposite}$); \texttt{face}
and \texttt{cell} are pure aggregate readouts. Two engine primitives generate everything:
\texttt{upcast\_*} (gather a face/cell value onto its half-edges $=$ \textbf{broadcast}) and
\texttt{sum\_*}/\texttt{mean\_*} (scatter-reduce half-edges onto a vert/face/cell $=$
\textbf{aggregate}). Geometry (\texttt{update\_all}) is a feed-forward aggregate/broadcast DAG;
mechanics is an \emph{additive superposition} of effector forces onto one \texttt{vert.force}
accumulator; a \emph{solver} integrates it; an \emph{event manager} schedules structural changes.

\paragraph{On naming --- a deciphering note.} tyssue's central methods are
\texttt{compute\_energy}, \texttt{compute\_gradient}, \texttt{find\_energy\_min},
\texttt{update\_all}. \emph{None} is a Plexus operator. plexus2 names an operator by the biological
\emph{mechanism} it performs (the contract: \texttt{line\_tension}, \texttt{area\_elasticity},
\texttt{reconnect\_T1}, \texttt{divide\_cell}), and names the numerics separately as an
\texttt{implementation}. A generic numerical verb is therefore a \emph{signal} that one has found
not an operator but a \emph{composition} (\texttt{compute\_energy} $=$ the additive superposition
of the twelve mechanics contracts), a \emph{solver} (\texttt{find\_energy\_min} $=$ an
implementation of \texttt{force\_balance}), or a \emph{readout pipeline} (\texttt{update\_all} $=$
the geometry aggregate/broadcast DAG). The extraction's job is precisely to replace these verbs
with mechanism-named contracts.

\paragraph{Saturation.} The mechanics \emph{saturate} against the existing algebra --- the
shape-energy family is already in Plexus and tyssue merely adds (analytic-gradient)
implementations. The genuinely new contributions are in \emph{topology}, \emph{numerics} and
\emph{structure}. This is exactly the plexus2 prediction: a mature simulator contributes mostly
implementations of existing contracts, plus a few new operators where the language was thin.

The two boldfaced new contracts --- \texttt{reconnect\_T1} and \texttt{force\_balance} --- are
exactly the pair the Turing--vertex report named as the tubulation bottleneck. They are not
incidental: they are what a \emph{true} vertex model has and a Voronoi one cannot.

\section{Stage 2: explicit topology --- T1, division, extrusion}
The operators the Voronoi route could not have live in a forked module
(\texttt{tyssue\_topology\_ops.py}, so the mechanics and the topology can evolve separately):
\texttt{t1\_transition} (rewire; reversible network reconnection = collapse a sub-threshold edge
and split the merged vertex), \texttt{face\_divide} (structural; a septum splits a cell),
\texttt{face\_extrude} (structural; collapse a face to a point), and \texttt{face\_growth}
(structural; inflate the per-cell target area). They mutate the face-ring topology carried on the
vertex \texttt{Level} and rebuild the flat half-edge table that \texttt{shape\_energy} reads.

\paragraph{Robustness contract.} Every topology mutation is applied to a trial copy, the affected
faces are validated (three-plus unique vertices, positive signed area), and the change is
\emph{committed only if valid} --- otherwise it is a silent no-op. The mesh therefore never
tangles from a bad flip, which matters for unattended runs. A standalone self-test on the
honeycomb confirms it: T1 fired on $30/40$ shortest edges (the $10$ skips are boundary /
non-three-regular edges, correctly refused), four divisions and one extrusion all executed, and
\emph{zero} invalid faces resulted.

\paragraph{In-engine demonstration.} Running \texttt{mesh\_seed} $\to$ \texttt{face\_divide} $\to$
\texttt{shape\_energy} $\to$ \texttt{t1\_transition} through the engine, a one-shot clonal
division of $25\%$ of the cells followed by relaxation grows the tissue from $162$ to $198$ cells
and \emph{stays force-balanced with zero invalid faces} (Fig.~\ref{fig:clonal}). The division
operator is a genuine \texttt{plexus2} structural operator on the vertex set, and the sheet
re-relaxes around each new junction.

\section{The two-level hierarchy: why \texttt{plexus2}, not just \texttt{tyssue}}
tyssue stores the whole tissue in one flat half-edge dataframe; a ``cell'' there is a \emph{row of
derived geometry}. \texttt{plexus2} instead makes the cell a first-class biological \emph{set}
carrying its \emph{own} state --- target area $a_0$, type/fate \texttt{ctype}, and (Goal~2)
morphogen \texttt{chem} --- none of which is derivable from vertex positions. The model then has
two Levels, \texttt{vertex} and \texttt{cell}, joined by the half-edge map, with a genuine
\texttt{cell\_geometry} \emph{Aggregate} (vertices\,$\to$\,cell area/perimeter) and a
\emph{cross-set} \texttt{shape\_energy} that reads $a_0$ from the cell set and broadcasts the force
back to the vertices (\texttt{INPUTS=[vertex, cell]}).

The benefit is concrete (Fig.~\ref{fig:clone}), and it must be done \emph{as biology, not as a
hack}. A first draft assigned fate by fiat --- a \texttt{cell\_paint} operator marked every cell
inside a hard-coded disc as a different type with a bigger target area. That proved the plumbing
but it is not a mechanism, so it was retired. In its place: a \texttt{cell\_morphogen} operator
imposes a smooth \emph{activator} field on the cell state \texttt{chem} (positional information ---
a Gaussian bump), and a \texttt{morphogen\_growth} operator makes each cell's target area a
\emph{continuous} response to it,
\[
a_0 \;=\; a_0^{\text{base}}\big(\rho + g\,\mathrm{Hill}(a)\big),\qquad
\mathrm{Hill}(a)=\frac{a^{n}}{a^{n}+a_{\text{sw}}^{n}} ,
\]
the Turing--vertex growth coupling, now acting on the cell set. The cross-set \texttt{shape\_energy}
reads $a_0$ and the tissue \emph{bulges where the activator is high}. The \emph{same} morphogen
field composes with mechanics in two ways, both principled: a \emph{continuous} response
(\texttt{morphogen\_growth}, above) gives a graded bulge whose cell area tracks the activator with
correlation $0.97$; and a \emph{discrete} response (\texttt{cell\_differentiate}) thresholds the
activator into a genuine cell \emph{type} --- a French-flag fate --- so the cells above threshold
become a coherent \emph{clone} (Fig.~\ref{fig:clone}) with a larger target area that bulges to
$2.6\times$ the wild-type, all force-balanced. In tyssue either coupling would be bespoke glue; here
each is \emph{state on a set} plus a typed operator (\texttt{morphogen $\to$ growth}, or
\texttt{morphogen $\to$ threshold $\to$ typed mechanics}), and it is exactly the substrate Goal~2
needs --- swap the imposed bump for a live Turing reaction--diffusion on the cell--cell adjacency and
the clone becomes a \emph{self-organised, activator-driven bud}. That composability (mechanism $+$
mechanism through a shared set/operator language) is the payoff of decomposing tyssue into
\texttt{plexus2} rather than wrapping it.

\paragraph{Straight-line (oriented) division (06).}
The placeholder through-vertex split (which degraded after $1$--$2$ rounds) is replaced by the
proper Brodland--Veldhuis division at a \emph{decidable angle}: the plane through a cell's centroid
cuts its two crossed edges, a new vertex is inserted on each (shared with the neighbour across that
edge), and a septum joins them --- well-shaped daughters, each targeting \emph{half} the mother's
area (area conserved, as tyssue halves \texttt{prefered\_area}). Fig.~\ref{fig:divide} divides
$35\%$ of a relaxed sheet at a fixed angle $\pi/2$ and re-relaxes: $118\!\to\!159$ cells, $0$ invalid
faces, the oriented (vertical) septa clearly visible --- the decidable division plane the demo is
about.

\paragraph{Apoptosis / cell elimination (B) + reversible network reconnection (A-RNR).}
The \texttt{apoptosis} operator shrinks the target area of a chosen cell each tick (so
\texttt{shape\_energy} contracts it) and, once it is small, eliminates it. A naive one-step
extrusion --- collapse the whole cell to a point --- leaves its six neighbours on a single vertex,
a degree-$6$ \emph{rosette} the three-regular T1 cannot relax (and collapsing a near-zero sliver is
numerically unstable). The fix is \emph{multistep} reconnection (Okuda's RNR; the A-RNR notebook, in
2D): while the cell is small it \emph{sheds a side} by a T1 on its shortest edge
($6\!\to\!5\!\to\!4\!\to\!3$), and only the resulting \emph{triangle} is collapsed --- to a clean
degree-$3$ junction. Cell (cortical) \emph{contractility} $\Gamma$ and line tension $\Lambda$ keep
the shrinking cell and its neighbours round rather than spiky (the neighbours \emph{constrict in},
apically closing the gap). With that, the cell is eliminated ($56\!\to\!55$, $0$ invalid faces) and
the tissue \emph{heals}: the neighbours reconnect and relax back to epithelium, no rosette
(Fig.~\ref{fig:apop}, the dying cell drawn dark grey); the only residue is the local topological defect
inherent to removing a cell from a hexagonal packing. This closes B-Apoptosis \emph{and} the 2D case
of A-RNR; the 3D IH/HI reconnections belong to the 3D-vesicle stage.

\paragraph{Integration test: a growing, dividing tissue (and why surface tension matters).}
The operators compose into a developing tissue: \texttt{face\_growth} inflates each cell's target
area, \texttt{face\_divide\_line} in cell-cycle mode divides any cell past a threshold at a random
angle (edge-midpoint, well-shaped daughters each targeting half), \texttt{shape\_energy} holds force
balance, and \texttt{t1\_transition} resolves short junctions. On a \emph{free} (unpinned) boundary
the sheet expands as it grows. Three settings proved essential. (i)~A free boundary, so growth is not
frustrated into over-compression. (ii)~\emph{Line tension} $\Lambda\sum_e\ell_e$ --- without which the
dividing cells relax into sharp triangular slivers and the free rim spikes into points; it smooths the
boundary and straightens junctions. (iii)~\emph{Cortical contractility} $\tfrac12\Gamma\sum_f P_f^2$
--- a per-cell perimeter penalty that rounds each daughter out between divisions instead of letting it
elongate; without it the tissue drifts uniformly fluid (shape index far above $p_0$) and cells
sharpen. With line tension \emph{and} contractility the daughters stay round, well-shaped polygons at
a moderate shape index (this pair also smooths the apoptosis reconnection). The tissue proliferates
$88\!\to\!176$ cells with $0$ invalid faces --- \emph{sustained} edge-midpoint division
(Fig.~\ref{fig:grow}), in contrast to the Stage-2 through-vertex placeholder that tangled after
$1$--$2$ rounds.

\section{Goal 1bis: the 3D vesicle (surface vertex model)}
The same operators lift to 3D. A closed spherical half-edge mesh --- the spherical Voronoi of a
Fibonacci sphere, a Goldberg polyhedron of hexagons and exactly twelve pentagons, with Euler
characteristic $V\!-\!E\!+\!F=2$ and every edge shared by two faces --- carries the AVM in three
dimensions. \texttt{shape\_energy\_3d} uses the 3D geometry (a Newell area vector per face, 3D edge
lengths) plus a \emph{lumen-volume} term $K_V(V-V_0)^2$ that keeps the shell inflated against surface
tension --- the closure a 2D patch got from pinning. The force is one 3D autograd pass; the bounded
Euler step carries over unchanged.

One 3D-specific lesson earned its keep. A free surface with area $+$ tension energy and \emph{no
bending rigidity} \emph{buckles} out of plane (roughness $0.004\!\to\!0.155$) --- exactly as a real
epithelium does; the lumen term fixes the total volume but not local smoothness. Adding a soft
radial term $K_R\sum_v(|\mathbf r_v|-R_0)^2$ (a bending-like penalty toward the shell) recovers a
smooth relaxed vesicle: the roughness stays $\sim\!0.02$ and the cross-section stays circular
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ves}). This closed, force-balanced shell is the substrate for 3D morphogen-driven
budding --- the 3D IH/HI reconnections (the 3D half of A-RNR) and a mechanical monolayer (apical/basal,
with \texttt{cell\_volume} elasticity) are the remaining 3D increments.

The vesicle is rendered as a \emph{monolayer}: each cell is drawn as a prism --- an apical face
(outer), a basal face (inner, the surface scaled radially inward), and the lateral walls between ---
so a cell carries many faces, not one flat polygon. The apical faces share the mesh vertices and tile
edge-to-edge into a closed opaque shell; cells take the Turing white$\to$red activation LUT (here
activation $=0$, all white, awaiting the Goal 2 reaction--diffusion). The cross-section slices the
monolayer: each junction edge crossing the plane gives an apical point and its inward-scaled basal
counterpart, and the connecting segment is that junction's lateral wall, partitioning the shell into
the individual cells around the lumen (Fig.~\ref{fig:ves}, bottom).

\subsection*{Growth by per-cell volume elasticity (the emergent inflation)}
Growing the vesicle turned out to be the sharpest model-deciphering lesson of the 3D stage. The naive
recipe --- ramp each cell's target area $A_0$ and a single \emph{global} lumen volume $V_0$, then let
the shape energy inflate the shell --- \emph{fails}: the sphere lags its targets and the surplus cell
area buckles out of plane into deep crumples (roughness $0.01\!\to\!0.2$, shape index climbing from
$3.8$ to $4.4$ --- ``too much fabric on a ball''). Neither slowing the growth, adding relaxation, nor
strengthening a global lumen pump fixed it, because a \emph{single scalar} $K_V(V-V_0)^2$ constrains
only the \emph{sum} of the cell volumes: any set of local dips that cancel in the sum is energetically
free, so buckling is unopposed.

The fix is the mechanism both references already use. tyssue's \texttt{ClosedMonolayer} gives every
\emph{cell} a \texttt{prefered\_vol} and \texttt{vol\_elasticity} (not just a lumen), and
Turing\_vertex's Eq.~3 writes the cell energy as $U=\sum_j\big[\tfrac12 k_v(v_j-v_{\mathrm{eq},j})^2
+\kappa_s s_j\big]$ --- a \emph{per-cell} volume elastic energy. So we split the one global lumen term
into a sum over cells: the lumen volume $V=\sum_f v_f$ with per-cell wedge volumes
$v_f=\tfrac13(\mathbf{c}_f\!\cdot\!\mathbf N_f)$ (the pyramid from the sphere centre to face $f$), and
the energy carries $\sum_f K_V\,(v_f-v_{\mathrm{eq},f})^2$. Now each cell holds \emph{its own} volume:
a local dip lowers that cell's $v_f$ and is penalised, so the term \emph{actively resists buckling},
and growth is just ramping each $v_{\mathrm{eq},f}$ (with $A_0\propto s^2$, $v_{\mathrm{eq}}\propto
s^3$). The whole shell then inflates purely by force balance --- no vertex is moved by hand --- from
radius $\sim\!5$ to $\sim\!7.1$ while staying smooth (roughness $\le 0.01$) and keeping the cells at
their target shape (index $3.8\!\to\!3.87$), Fig.~\ref{fig:vesgrow}. The lesson: \emph{distributed}
elastic targets, not a global constraint, are what make morphogenetic expansion emerge and stay
stable --- the same reason real epithelia use cell-autonomous volume control.

\subsection*{Division in 3D (a proliferating vesicle)}
The last 3D increment is cell \emph{division} on the closed shell --- the sphere analog of tyssue's
\texttt{sheet\_topology.cell\_division} (a face split by a line; the bulk \texttt{monolayer\_topology}
version with a \texttt{vertical}/\texttt{horizontal} orientation waits for the mechanical monolayer).
The cell cycle is \emph{volume doubling}: each cell records its birth wedge volume and divides once its
current volume reaches twice that (\texttt{divide\_3d}). A division splits the cell by an
\emph{edge-midpoint septum} --- two of its edges are cut at their midpoints ($m_1,m_2$) and a new edge
$m_1$--$m_2$ separates the daughters. The subtlety unique to 3D: every edge of a closed surface is
shared by two faces, so cutting an edge \emph{also} inserts the midpoint into the neighbouring cell's
ring. Getting that bookkeeping right keeps the mesh a valid closed surface: each division adds exactly
$\Delta V\!=\!2,\ \Delta E\!=\!3,\ \Delta F\!=\!1$, so $V\!-\!E\!+\!F$ is invariant. A standalone check
(reorienting the SphericalVoronoi outward first, so every directed edge is unique) confirms
Euler $=2$ across dozens of divisions; \emph{in the engine} the growing, dividing vesicle proliferates
$150\!\to\!521$ cells over $371$ divisions in $220$ frames and still verifies \texttt{closed = true,
Euler = 2} (Fig.~\ref{fig:vesdiv}). Pacing follows the Turing\_vertex \texttt{fig4\_vesicle} reference
(volume-doubling, a few divisions per frame) so the tissue proliferates gradually. Two subtleties were
worth fixing: divisions must be chosen \emph{unbiased} --- selecting the first ready cells in face-index
order sweeps pole-to-pole because the Fibonacci seeding numbers faces that way, so we shuffle the
candidates and randomise the septum direction --- and daughters must be kept \emph{compact}. On the
last point we followed a literature sweep (tyssue's \texttt{monolayer\_topology}, Turing\_vertex, cellGPU):
the emergent fix is not a smoothing pass but two mechanisms already in the physics. (i)~\emph{Long-axis}
(Hertwig) division --- the septum cuts perpendicular to the cell's longest axis (an SVD of the cell's
vertices), which yields compact daughters instead of elongated slivers. (ii)~\emph{Cortical
contractility} $\tfrac12\Gamma\sum_f P_f^2$, the perimeter term that rounds each cell. Together they
drop the shape index from $\sim\!4.2$ to $\sim\!3.9$ \emph{emergently} (a geometric Lloyd smoothing
would also round the cells, but it is a fairing hack, not physics, so we discarded it). The last step
toward the regular-hexagon floor is \emph{T1 neighbour exchange}, the annealing mechanism, now added as
\texttt{reconnect\_t1\_3d} (a \textbf{Rewire} op, the 3D sibling of the 2D \texttt{t1\_transition}):
every interior edge shorter than a threshold is flipped by a local four-cell neighbour exchange
($A$--$B$ lose the edge, the far cells $C$--$D$ gain it), collapsing the edge to its midpoint and
reopening it perpendicular in the tangent plane. It keeps $V,E,F$ (and Euler) fixed and only reconnects;
a standalone check confirms the shell stays closed with Euler$=2$ through $200+$ flips. Adding it to the
dividing vesicle anneals the division defects and drops the shape index a further $3.96\!\to\!3.88$.
Note the sphere mandates $\ge\!12$ pentagonal defects (Euler), so perfect hexagons are not the target. \emph{At scale} (the fig4 test: $600\!\to\!2367$ cells over $1000$ frames) the shell stays
\texttt{closed, Euler}$=2$, roughness $0.008$, shape index $3.84$ --- rounder at scale, as more cells
anneal better.

\section{Goal 2: Turing reaction--diffusion on the cell set}
The two-level hierarchy pays off again for morphogenesis. The morphogen lives on the \emph{cell} set as
state \texttt{chem}$=[a,h]$ (a first-class biological DOF, not derivable from vertex geometry), while
the mechanical \emph{vertex} set holds the shell. Three operators run the reaction--diffusion each
frame: \texttt{cell\_geometry\_3d} (an \textbf{Aggregate}: vertices $\to$ per-cell centroid),
\texttt{cell\_adjacency} (a \textbf{Rewire}: the cell--cell neighbour graph) and
\texttt{cell\_diffuse}\,$+$\,\texttt{cell\_react} (\textbf{Lateral} field updates that integrate
\texttt{chem}).

Two points are worth stating. First, \emph{how we get cell neighbours without a Voronoi}: in the AVM
the cells \emph{are} the faces of the half-edge mesh, so two cells are neighbours iff they share a mesh
\emph{edge} --- \texttt{cell\_adjacency} groups the half-edges by their vertex pair and links the two
faces that share each edge. The mesh topology already \emph{is} the adjacency graph; no Delaunay/Voronoi
of cell centres is needed (that is only how the Self-Propelled-Voronoi sibling builds \emph{its} graph).
Second, \texttt{react} is a plexus2 \emph{contract} with interchangeable implementations: on the same
cell set, \texttt{gray\_scott} gives a coral/labyrinth and \texttt{brusselator} (its parameters
transposed verbatim from Turing\_vertex \texttt{fig4\_coral}: $\gamma=2,A=1,B=3$; $d_a=0.05, d_h=0.7$)
gives round Turing spots. The Brusselator's fast-inhibitor diffusion is only stable at the fig4
timestep $\mathrm{d}t=0.02$, so the whole sim runs at that step and \texttt{shape\_energy\_3d} is given
the same $\mathrm{d}t$ so the mechanics still relaxes fully each frame. A self-organised activator
pattern emerges across the closed shell (Fig.~\ref{fig:coral}), coloured white$\to$red.

Crucially the pattern couples to \emph{topological change}. By construction \texttt{rd\_coral\_grow}
is \emph{exactly the dividing vesicle of Fig.~\ref{fig:vesdiv} plus the coral colouring}: its mechanical
schedule (growth $\to$ shape energy $\to$ T1 $\to$ division) is that of \texttt{vesicle\_divide}
verbatim, and the reaction--diffusion ops only \emph{read} the mesh (aggregate vertices to cell
centroids, integrate \texttt{chem}) --- they never move a vertex. We verified that the hard way: a
controlled triple at identical mechanics --- \texttt{grow\_only}, \texttt{grow\_divide}, and
\texttt{grow\_divide}$+$RD --- gave \emph{byte-identical vertex trajectories} for the last two, so the
morphogen is provably pure colouring. The one genuine coupling runs the other way:
\texttt{divide\_3d} propagates the mother's \texttt{chem} to its daughters (the kept face retains the
mother's state, the new face inherits a copy), so the Turing field rides the proliferating tissue
coherently instead of being scrambled by division. At the vesicle\_divide scale ($150\!\to\!518$ cells)
the labyrinth develops cleanly on the growing, dividing shell.

\paragraph{The synchronised division wave, and the stochastic cell cycle that breaks it.} Scaling the
same recipe up --- to $500$ cells over $1000$ frames, for a finer pattern --- exposed a real
instability, and it took the controlled experiment above to show it was \emph{not} the
reaction--diffusion. Because every cell is born at the same volume and grows uniformly, they all cross
the volume-doubling threshold \emph{in lockstep}: the cell count climbs in flat $+100$-per-frame steps
(a wave capped only by \texttt{max\_div}), so the shell is driven under sustained \emph{max-rate}
division for the entire growth ramp. Fresh, unrelaxed daughters accumulate faster than the bounded
Euler relaxation can smooth them and the (face-referenced) shell roughness at mid-ramp blows up to
$0.38$ (worst frame $1.7$) --- the transient ``grey'' of exposed cell walls --- annealing back only
once growth plateaus. The fix is the one real epithelia use: \emph{cell-cycle-time variability}. Two
knobs make the division \emph{stochastic}: a \emph{stochastic volume seed} (\texttt{vseed\_cv} on
\texttt{seed\_mesh\_3d}) gives each cell a random initial cell-cycle phase so the \emph{first} wave is
desynchronised, and a \emph{stochastic cell cycle} (\texttt{cycle\_cv} on \texttt{divide\_3d}) draws
each daughter's next division threshold from a Gaussian so later generations stay spread out. With
CV$=0.4$ the lockstep wave becomes a smooth ramp (cells climb gradually from frame $0$), the mid-ramp
roughness drops two orders of magnitude ($0.38\!\to\!0.004$), and \texttt{rd\_coral\_grow\_big}
proliferates $500\!\to\!1994$ cells while staying smooth throughout --- a finer coral labyrinth on the
larger shell (Fig.~\ref{fig:coralgrow}). The lesson repeats the document's refrain: the artefact
\emph{looked} like a shading or reaction--diffusion bug, but it was a \emph{synchronisation} property
of the mechanics, cured by restoring the biological noise a deterministic cell cycle had omitted.

\paragraph{The ``hollow cells'' were a recording artefact, not a physical defect (2026-07-21).} A long
\texttt{rd\_coral\_grow} (and any dividing vesicle past $\sim\!500$ cells) rendered a shell the
\texttt{hollow\_flags} diagnostic scored as $\sim\!97\%$ ``hollow'' (folded/inverted caps) --- a striking
grey/red crumpling that annealed away by the final frame. A long chain of controlled experiments
established, in order, that it was division-driven, that a \emph{single} division is geometrically clean
(the Hertwig septum midpoints land \emph{on} the surface and both daughters' normals stay parallel to the
mother, $\mathbf{n}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}_{\text{mother}}=0.995,\,0.998$), and that \emph{no} physical remedy
touched it --- an anti-inversion filtered step, local daughter relaxation, dihedral bending to
$K_{\text{bend}}=100$, removing the radial term, tripling the relaxation, even slowing growth (which was
\emph{worse}). That every plausible mechanical fix failed was the tell: there was no mechanical defect.

The cause was a \textbf{recording misalignment}. The engine records vertex \emph{positions}
(\texttt{posf}) at one stride while \texttt{topo\_snapshot\_3d} records the changing \emph{mesh topology}
(\texttt{hist}) at another; on runs long enough to trip a recording stride $>1$ the two arrays end up
\emph{different lengths} (at $300$ frames \texttt{posf} has $151$ entries, \texttt{hist} $76$). The
diagnostic and renderer then paired positions from one frame with the half-edge connectivity of
\emph{another} --- ring vertex indices no longer matched the coordinates, every Newell normal was computed
from a scrambled polygon, and the metric reported phantom inversions. The proof is one line: the identical
$300$-frame run with \texttt{topo\_snapshot every}$=2$ (\texttt{posf}$=151$, \texttt{hist}$=76$,
\emph{misaligned}) scores hollow $0.98$; with \texttt{every}$=1$ (both $301$, \emph{aligned}) it scores
$\mathbf{0.000}$ throughout. The $220$-frame vesicle looked clean only because at that length the stride was
$1$ and the recordings happened to stay aligned. Recording topology every frame the positions are recorded
makes \texttt{vesicle\_grow\_divide} a smooth sphere all the way to $949$ cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:hollow}).
The lesson is the document's oldest refrain, sharpened: \emph{measure the geometric thing you mean} --- a
diagnostic that silently pairs two arrays of different provenance will fabricate a defect that no amount of
physics can fix. (One genuine, mild residue survives the fix: with a deterministic cell cycle the cells
divide in backlogged waves, so their \emph{areas} spread; the stochastic cell cycle CV$=0.4$ plus a
live-scaled division cap even them out, as \texttt{rd\_coral\_grow\_big} already showed. The earlier
\emph{roughness}-metric ``synchronised wave'' result is unaffected --- that metric reads positions only.)

\paragraph{Cell-size homogenisation --- a hypothesis-driven fix (2026-07-22).} On the growing, dividing
vesicle the cells were too non-uniform (per-cell area CV $\approx\!0.6$; the tube-tip cell volume was
$1.5\times$ the base with a huge spread, $1.56\pm2.45$ vs $1.07\pm0.35$). Rather than a grid search, three
falsifiable hypotheses were tested one factor at a time (short $150$-cell/$180$-frame A/Bs, metric $=$
per-cell area CV). \emph{H1 --- grow by proliferation, not inflation:} \texttt{vesicle\_growth} enlarges
the shell by ramping every cell's target volume (cells bulge), whereas Okuda grows by \emph{proliferation}
--- capping $v_{eq}$ at $\tfrac43 v_{ref}$ so each cell cycles in $[\tfrac23,\tfrac43]v_{ref}$ and the
sphere grows by cell \emph{count}. Switching to \texttt{morphogen\_growth\_3d} with baseline $\rho=1$ and
the cap cut area CV $0.63\!\to\!0.40$ (validated). \emph{H2 --- tighten division timing:} the dominant
residue was the loose division-threshold jitter (CV$_{\text{cycle}}=0.4$); tightening to $0.15$ and
\emph{bounding the cell-cycle duration} (\texttt{min\_cycle}$=4$, \texttt{max\_cycle}$=12$) dropped area
CV $0.40\!\to\!0.18$ --- the biggest single step. \emph{H3 --- stiffen volume elasticity:} raising
$K_V\,1\!\to\!4$ so cells track $v_{eq}$ took area CV to $\mathbf{0.17}$ (Okuda-uniform range) and the
hollow fraction to $\mathbf{0}$: a spotless, uniform shell. The division model is now Okuda's ---
\emph{volume-primary} ($2\times$ birth volume) \emph{with a bounded cell-cycle duration} --- the two
competing timing models parameterised together. This uniform substrate is the prerequisite for the
multi-tube morphogenesis, and it is reached by short, hypothesis-driven steps rather than a blind sweep.

\paragraph{RD on the homogenised shell --- ``pure colouring'' is only statistical (2026-07-22).}
Before coupling the Turing activator to growth we sanity-checked the usual claim that the
reaction--diffusion is \emph{pure colouring}. Adding the Brusselator RD to the homogenised recipe (it
develops a clean pattern, activator range $0.36$--$4.42$) leaves the morphology \emph{statistically}
unchanged --- same cell count ($1001$), same area CV ($0.198$ vs $0.199$), same spotless mesh --- yet the
vertex trajectories \emph{fully diverge} ($\max|\Delta\mathbf{x}|=19$), so it is \emph{not} byte-identical.
The reason is instructive and was flagged by an inconsistency check: \texttt{morphogen\_growth\_3d}
\emph{reads} the activator, so even a deliberately inert coupling (\texttt{a\_sw}$=50$, so the growth
drive is $1\!\to\!1.00006$) is a $6\times10^{-5}$ perturbation, and a growing, dividing tissue is
\emph{chaotic} --- that nudge amplifies over $400$ frames into an unrelated but statistically identical
mesh. (The earlier \texttt{rd\_coral} ``byte-identical'' result held only because \emph{its} growth
operator, \texttt{vesicle\_growth}, never read the activator.) The lesson for the metric ledger:
``pure colouring'' must be qualified as \emph{statistical} whenever the growth operator consumes the
field, and trajectory-identity is simply the wrong invariant in a chaotic regime --- agreement of
cell count and area CV is the right one.

This is the substrate for the morphogenesis proper --- morphogen-driven growth (bulge/divide where $a$
is high) on a \emph{fluid} ($p_0\!>\!3.81$) shell where T1 lets the tissue flow, the bridge to tubes.

\section{Gap analysis: Okuda's model vs.\ our prototype (2026-07-23)}
Having re-read Okuda \emph{et al.}\ (2018, \emph{Sci.\ Rep.}\ \textbf{8}:2386) in full, we can now list
exactly which of its mechanisms our prototype already carries, which it implements differently, and which
are genuinely \emph{missing} and would need a new operator. This is the map for the remaining work.

\paragraph{Okuda's recipe in one place.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Mechanics} (Eq.~1,3): overdamped vertex dynamics $\eta(\dot r - V_f)=-\nabla U$, iterated to
force balance (residual $<E_{\text{th}}$). Energy $U=\sum_j\big[\tfrac12 k_v(v_j-v_{\text{eq}j})^2+\kappa_s
s_j\big]$ --- per-cell \emph{volume elasticity} $+$ \emph{linear surface tension}. Cells are true \emph{3D
polyhedra}: each has its own volume $v_j$ and surface area $s_j$.
\item \textbf{Reaction--diffusion} (Eq.~2): Gierer--Meinhardt, transport $=(D/\text{gap})\times(\text{boundary
area})$ on the cell graph. $\chi$ (diffusion coeff.)\ sets spot size $=$ tube diameter ($\propto\chi^{1/4}$).
\item \textbf{Growth} (Eq.~8): $\lambda_j=\lambda_{\text{ref}j}\,\mathrm{Hill}(\rho/\rho_{\text{sw}})^\alpha$
grows the \emph{equilibrium volume} $v_{\text{eq}j}$; $\lambda_{\text{ref}}$ is a random Gaussian per cell.
\item \textbf{Division}: plane normal to the long axis, \emph{in the tissue surface} (oriented, in-plane).
\item \textbf{Reconnection}: RNR/T1 when an edge falls below $\Delta l_{\text{th}}$.
\item $\boldsymbol\gamma$: the \emph{ratio of patterning to deformation timescales} (the regime knob ---
$\gamma{=}1$ tubes, $\gamma{=}100$ undulation, $\gamma{=}0.01$ no steady pattern). Stability under growth
comes from ``the intrinsic \emph{hysteresis} in the activator--inhibitor system with domain growth.''
\item \textbf{Bending is emergent}: tube straightness follows from diameter (thin undulate, thick straight);
there is \emph{no} explicit bending energy.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A. Already matched.} Force-balance relaxation; Gierer--Meinhardt RD; diffusion on the cell
adjacency; Hill-function growth of $v_{\text{eq}}$; oriented in-plane (Hertwig) division; T1/RNR; per-cell
volume elasticity ($K_V$).

\paragraph{B. Implemented differently --- needs modification.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Energy form.} Okuda $=$ volume elasticity $+$ \emph{linear} surface tension $\kappa_s s$ only. Ours
carries area/perimeter \emph{targets} $+$ contractility $+$ line tension $+$ a wedge-volume. $\Rightarrow$ add
a linear surface-tension term; make the area/perimeter targets optional.
\item \emph{Diffusion weighting.} Okuda weights transport by boundary-area/gap-width (geometric); ours is a
plain graph Laplacian. $\Rightarrow$ weight \texttt{cell\_diffuse} by shared-edge length $/$ cell volume.
\item $\gamma$ \emph{as a clean knob} --- expose patterning-rate $\div$ deformation-rate explicitly (we
approximate it with \texttt{rd\_rate}).
\item \emph{Random per-cell growth rate} ($\lambda_{\text{ref}}$ Gaussian) --- minor add.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{C. Missing --- needs a new operator.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{True 3D cell geometry (the big one).} Okuda's monolayer is a \emph{shell of 3D cells}: each has
an apical $+$ basal surface and a \emph{thickness}, hence its own 3D volume $v_j=\text{area}\times\text{height}$
and 3D surface $s_j$ (apical$+$basal$+$lateral). Our model is a single mid-surface with a \emph{lumen-wedge}
volume (volume from the sphere centre, not the cell's own volume). $\Rightarrow$ a new \emph{monolayer
geometry}: apical$+$basal surfaces (a thickness field) giving per-cell 3D volume and surface. This is what
growth actually inflates in Okuda.
\item \textbf{Surface-tension energy on the 3D cell surface} ($\kappa_s s$) --- depends on C1.
\item \textbf{Emergent bending} (the $\chi\to$ straight/undulate phenomenology) --- \emph{falls out} of C1
(monolayer thickness $\Rightarrow$ diameter-dependent bending rigidity). We have been substituting an
explicit $K_{\text{bend}}$; Okuda's is emergent, so C1 is the faithful route.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reading.} The \emph{immediate} blocker --- the activator flooding the shell under growth --- is
\emph{geometry-independent}: it is domain-growth pattern insertion (a Turing-unstable RD tiling the enlarging
shell), triggered by the basal source $a_0$ igniting the background. Fix that first (kill $a_0$; stay in a
localised-structure/hysteretic regime; saturation is the \emph{wrong} lever --- it converts spots to rings).
A basic tube can plausibly form in the single-surface model once the RD is confined and growth is localised,
because a sheet \emph{can} buckle into a finger. But Okuda's \emph{full} phenomenology --- constant diameter
set by $\chi$, thin-undulate/thick-straight --- \emph{requires} C1, the monolayer thickness. That is the
single highest-leverage new operator, and the planned next step once the flood is fixed.

\section*{Field notes}
\emph{Dated log of what actually worked or failed --- the hard-won part.}

\paragraph{How we proceed --- a hypothesis-driven, metric-gated method (2026-07-22).}
The morphogenesis stages are worked not by parameter grids but by \emph{small, falsifiable steps}. Each
step states a hypothesis, is measured by a \emph{dedicated metric whose analysis code is written on the
spot} (area CV for cell uniformity, a $95^{\text{th}}$-percentile/median radius for tube protrusion, the
hollow flag for mesh integrity), and is run \emph{cheap-first} (short local A/Bs) before any expensive
verification is dispatched to the cluster. Two disciplines earn their keep. \emph{One factor at a time:}
the homogenisation win was only attributable because H1/H2/H3 were isolated (proliferation-not-inflation,
then tight division timing, then stiff $K_V$). \emph{Inconsistency detection:} the sharpest debugging
tool is not distrust but a hunt for violations of expected invariants. The recording artefact surfaced
from one such contradiction --- a $300$-frame run blowing up while its own first $220$ frames (which
\emph{must} be identical by determinism) stayed clean; that impossibility, not a hunch, forced the
investigation that found the mis-aligned recording. A live example of the method policing itself: a tube
experiment ran suspiciously slower than its analog; the proposed cause (a \texttt{max\_cycle} duration
bound force-dividing the supposedly volume-locked body --- a parameter added for \emph{uniformity}
silently invalidating a different test's premise) was itself \emph{falsified} on re-run (still slow), and
the true cause traced to the large-cell bulge regime. Both the catch and the correction are recorded, so
the method's knowledge base evolves as hypotheses are validated or overturned rather than accreting
untested lore. (This log, the memory index, and the operator atlas together are that evolving knowledge
base; formalising it as an explicit, revisable \emph{master knowledge document} --- the memory of an
autonomous master controller over the three Plexus2 loops --- is the natural next step.)

\paragraph{Perfecting --- surface tension rounds the cells (2026-07-22).} A satisfied metric is not
qualitative fidelity. The homogenised recipe passed on uniformity (vol CV $\sim0.12$) yet the rendered
cells were visibly \emph{triangular} --- angular slivers thrown off by division and T1 --- where the
reference's Voronoi-propelled cells are round. No existing metric captured this, so we authored one: the
median shape index $P/\sqrt{A}$ (a regular hexagon is $3.72$; slivers are higher) and the fraction of
cells with $\le4$ sides. Hypothesis H10: more \emph{surface tension} (line tension $\Lambda$ and cortical
contractility $\Gamma$, with a softer volume stiffness $K_V$ and $p_0\!=\!3.5$) rounds the cells. It
validated cleanly --- shape index $3.94\!\to\!3.81$, sliver fraction $0.035\!\to\!0.017$ (halved) --- at
a small, acceptable uniformity cost (vol CV $0.17\!\to\!0.21$). The two clean archives
(\texttt{vh\_K4\_cv15\_d4} and its coral twin) were re-rendered with the rounded parameters. This is
\emph{perfecting}: a qualitative gap the eye flags, turned into a measurable target and closed --- the
mechanism by which the goal sharpens as the model approaches the reference, and the discipline a hurried
operator most often skips.

\paragraph{Cluster submission --- never trust a submit's return (2026-07-22).} Moving the Fig-5-scale
runs ($\sim2000$ cells) to a dedicated L4 node freed the devcontainer, but exposed a false-completion
trap. The \texttt{gpu\_l4} queue rejects jobs without an explicit \texttt{-gpu num=1}; more subtly, the
login link is laggy, so a \texttt{bsub} that \emph{succeeds} can return a truncated reply that reads as
``$0$ jobs submitted'' --- and, symmetrically, a submit that appears to \emph{time out} may have already
queued the job (we accidentally created a duplicate this way). The only ground truth is the queue itself.
The fix, ported from the \texttt{connectome-gnn-cx} poller, is a retry-with-backoff \texttt{--wait}/%
\texttt{--status} loop that treats any non-empty \texttt{bjobs} output as authoritative and an empty
queue (or \texttt{DONE}/\texttt{EXIT}) as completion. The generalisable rule for the autonomous loop: an
action's \emph{reported} outcome is a hint; verify against the world's state before proceeding.

\paragraph{Scaling to Fig-5 size --- uniformity breaks with proliferation, not relaxation (2026-07-22).}
Taking the homogenised+rounded recipe to Okuda's $\sim2000$-cell start exposed a clean failure: seeding
$2000$ cells and running $220$ frames blew up to $7128$ cells with vol CV $1.63$ and hollow fraction
$0.33$ --- and the plain ($dt\!=\!1$) and coral ($dt\!=\!0.02$) runs failed \emph{identically to three
decimals}, which both falsified a $dt$/cell-cycle hypothesis and re-confirmed RD-as-colouring. Two
disciplined tests then localised the cause. A \emph{seed sweep} ($500/1000/1500/2000$ cells, fixed
frames) showed a monotonic degradation --- vol CV $0.37\!\to\!0.63\!\to\!0.86\!\to\!1.08$, hollow
$0.001\!\to\!0.04\!\to\!0.15\!\to\!0.22$ --- while the shape index stayed rounded ($\sim3.8$) throughout,
so surface tension holds but bulk uniformity does not. \emph{Fix diagnostics} at $n\!=\!2000$ then
falsified the two obvious mechanical cures: doubling the relaxation iterations ($30\!\to\!80$) and adding
radial anti-buckling stiffness ($K_R\!=\!2$) each left vol CV at $1.6$ --- untouched. Only \emph{bounding
the proliferation} (stopping at $\sim2400$ cells) recovered a clean shell (vol CV $0.51$, hollow $0.04$).
The lesson: the defect is set by the \emph{proliferation rate} (divisions per frame, which scales with
cell count), not by how well any single frame is relaxed --- the original clean recipe reached vol CV
$0.12$ at $1778$ cells precisely because $150$ seeds over $500$ frames divide gently. Fig~5's $2000$-cell
vesicle is therefore a \emph{start} to be grown into slowly, not a bulk to be proliferated $4\times$; the
next step grows to $2000$ from a small seed over many frames (and with a gentler per-frame division cap).

\paragraph{Tube coupling --- protrusion and hollowing are the same event, and the metric that revealed it
(2026-07-22).} The first attempt at RD-driven tubes on $2000$ cells blew up (a few giant cells, an
exploded cross-section) --- too many large changes at once. Decomposing on a small $150$-cell shell,
one variable at a time, gave the mechanism. \emph{Coupling ladder} (growth rate at a locked body): at
rate $0.03$ the shell stays clean but flat (protr $1.08$); at $0.06$ protrusion appears (protr $1.5$)
\emph{but the hollow count jumps from $2$ to $34$ in the same step}; at $0.12$+ it is strong protrusion
with catastrophic hollowing ($500$+). There is no clean-protrusion window because, with a volume-locked
body, an activated cell \emph{inflates faster than it can divide and balloons --- and a ballooned cell
cap is exactly what the hollow metric flags}. Protrusion-by-inflation \emph{is} hollowing. The natural
fix (make division keep pace so the tube grows by proliferation, not inflation) was then \emph{falsified}:
tripling division throughput made hollowing \emph{worse} ($20\!\to\!250$), not better --- more cells is
more mesh to tangle. (That ladder first showed every high-division run pinned at exactly $890$ cells --- a
buffer ceiling, not physics; caught and corrected before drawing any conclusion, the same
verify-the-instrument discipline as the recording artefact.) The contrast with the clean static-cone
tubes (protr $2.9$, hollow peak $12$) is instructive: the cones \emph{re-seed the activator at the tip
every frame}, so the activator tracks the growing tip (Okuda: ``the activator stayed around the tip'');
the RD spots sit where they nucleate and do not follow, so growth disorganises. This whole diagnosis was
only possible because the single end-frame hollow number ($0.004$, which the movie plainly contradicted)
was replaced with \emph{per-frame} metrics --- hollow count, size CV, and average tube diameter over time
--- authored precisely because the eye disagreed with the summary statistic.

\paragraph{Resolving the balloon --- activation width, checkpoint init, and a hard size cap (2026-07-22).}
Many small archived L4 rounds (each a strip + movie + metric plot) converged the tubing recipe. First,
\emph{H-narrow}: cones do not balloon because they activate a \emph{narrow} cone (few cells $\to$ narrow
tube), whereas an RD Turing spot activates a \emph{wide} patch (many cells $\to$ wide balloon). Raising the
growth switch \texttt{a\_sw} so only each spot's \emph{peak} drives growth cut the peak hollow count
$787\!\to\!50$ (16$\times$) while keeping protrusion --- but adding any baseline body growth ($\rho\!>\!0$)
re-flattened it, so a volume-locked body is mandatory. Second, a genuine \emph{mesh checkpoint}: the target
is morphogenesis \emph{on} a uniform vesicle, so we now save a homogenised run's full state (mesh topology
$+$ vertex positions $+$ cell activator field) and reload it as the initial condition (\texttt{load\_mesh\_3d},
a drop-in for the fresh-sphere seed). Initialising from that checkpoint and seeding a few big cone-spots
reproduces the intended starting picture exactly --- a uniform-celled shell with a handful of large red
foci --- which then grow tubes. Third, the tube-tip cells grew ``far too big'' because oversized cells
\emph{backlog behind the per-call division throttle} and keep ramping while queued; the fix is a
\emph{hard size cap} (\texttt{vcap}) that force-divides any cell above $v_{\mathrm{cap}}\!\cdot\!v_{\mathrm{ref}}$
\emph{bypassing} the throttle, so no cell --- tip included --- can exceed the cap. A methodological note
threaded through all of it: two rounds were nearly over-interpreted before catching confounds (a spot-size
sweep that actually tracked the RD CFL wall via \texttt{chi}; a division-throughput ladder pinned at a cell
\emph{buffer} ceiling), and one run's ``empty'' movie was a fixed camera zoomed out by a ballooned final
frame --- fixed with per-frame autoscale. Verify the instrument before trusting the measurement.

\paragraph{Tube emergence needs tip-riding, and the RD choice decides it (2026-07-23).} Pushing for
\emph{emergent} (not cone-prescribed) tubes forced a mechanism question: why does a \emph{flat} activation
spot not make a tube? Because growth adds area everywhere in the patch, so a uniformly-activated patch
inflates as a \emph{dome} (a spherical cap that \emph{widens}); a tube instead needs area added only at the
\emph{tip}, which then \emph{advances} --- i.e.\ the activation must \emph{ride the tip}. The cones tube
precisely because they re-seed at a fixed radial direction, so the growing radial tip stays inside the
cone; that is tip-riding done geometrically, and it is a hard-coded crutch. For it to \emph{emerge}, the
RD's activator peak must itself stay at the apex --- which is what the smooth activator gradient in Okuda's
figures is. So we studied the RD \emph{first, in isolation} (growth off, three big spots seeded, watch what
the RD does): the Brusselator is disqualified --- at low $\gamma$ it decays the seed to a uniform weak
background, at high $\gamma$ it overrides the seed with its own short-wavelength pattern that fills the
sphere, and either way its pattern forms only after $\sim$18\,s (the $1/\gamma$ time), far too late.
Gray--Scott in the \emph{spot} regime ($F\!=\!0.03$) was the only one of eight that held the three seeded
spots strong, localized and stable. Reading Okuda then closed the loop: their RD is neither of ours but the
\emph{Gierer--Meinhardt} activator--inhibitor --- a \emph{self-enhancing} activator with a fast lateral
inhibitor, whose hallmark is exactly a stable, self-maintaining activator \emph{peak with a gradient}. The
paper is explicit that the tube ``emerg[es] from the coupling of patterning and deformation'': the tip-%
riding is not hard-coded, it is a property of self-enhancing spot + growth co-evolving on the same
timescale. The plan follows directly: implement Gierer--Meinhardt, confirm it makes a stable gradient
peak, then couple growth and measure \texttt{tip\_act} (the activator--radius correlation) to test whether
the peak rides the tip on its own. A supporting metric note: a spot-count that thresholded at the
$70^{\text{th}}$ percentile reported ``101 spots'' for the clean Gray--Scott run where the eye saw three ---
the threshold sat in the noise; counting only cells above half-maximum fixed it. The eye and the metric
must agree before either is trusted.

\paragraph{Tubing --- five coupled requirements (2026-07-23).} The session converged on a compact
mechanistic account of what a tube actually needs. It is not one ingredient but five that must hold
together:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Localized, narrow activation --- not a wide/flat patch.} Flat activation grows the
\emph{whole} patch, so it inflates as a \emph{dome that widens}; a tube needs growth concentrated in a
narrow region.
\item \textbf{The activation must ride the tip.} Growth has to be added at the \emph{apex} and
\emph{advance} with it, or the result is a dome. Cones achieve this \emph{geometrically} (a fixed radial
direction the growing tip stays inside) --- a hard-coded crutch; the \emph{emergent} route is an RD peak
that self-maintains at the apex.
\item \textbf{The RD must hold a stable localized peak --- and the RD choice is decisive.} The
Brusselator \emph{decays or reorganizes} the seed (fails); Gray--Scott in the spot regime \emph{holds} the
spots (works); \emph{Gierer--Meinhardt} --- the activator--inhibitor Okuda actually uses (ref.~37) --- is
\emph{self-enhancing} and \emph{amplifies} the seed into a stable gradient peak (best: the $a^2/h$
autocatalysis is the amplification feedback).
\item \textbf{Growth must be graded ($\propto$ activator), not a binary switch.} Then the peak (tip)
grows \emph{fastest} and growth tapers down the tube --- Okuda's visible gradient. A binary switch grows
the activated patch uniformly $\to$ dome.
\item \textbf{Cells stay uniform via a hard size cap} (\texttt{vcap}, force-divide above
$v_{\mathrm{cap}}v_{\mathrm{ref}}$), \textbf{and the body must be volume-locked} ($\rho\!=\!0$) or body
inflation flattens the protrusion.
\end{enumerate}
The one open question, currently on the cluster: does the Gierer--Meinhardt peak ride the tip \emph{on its
own} (emergent) once coupled to graded growth --- grown from frame~0, and with a warmup that lets the
pattern stabilize first? The metric that answers it is \texttt{tip\_act}, the correlation between activator
and radius: high means the peak sits at the protruding tip (emergent, Okuda-like); low means it stayed at
the base (a dome). A high \texttt{tip\_act} would mean the tube reproduced \emph{emergently} rather than
with the geometric cone crutch --- the whole point of the exercise.

\paragraph{3D division at scale (2026-07-21) --- a synchronised division wave, not a rendering bug.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The controlled experiment that exonerated the reaction--diffusion.}
\texttt{rd\_coral\_grow} rendered a transient grey band mid-growth that looked like a broken coral or a
shading bug. The decisive test was to strip it to a triple at \emph{identical} mechanics ---
\texttt{grow\_only}, \texttt{grow\_divide}, and \texttt{grow\_divide}$+$RD --- and compare
\emph{vertex trajectories}. The last two were byte-identical, so the reaction--diffusion is provably
pure colouring; \texttt{grow\_only} was smooth, so the roughness came from \emph{division under growth},
at scale. A per-frame trace nailed it: the cell count climbs in flat $+100$/frame steps (all cells
doubling together, a wave capped by \texttt{max\_div}) and the face-referenced roughness spikes to
$0.38$ (worst $1.7$) across the whole ramp. What the eye reported as ``grey'' was a
\emph{synchronisation}, not a mesh error.
\item \textbf{Stochastic cell cycle: the biological noise the model had omitted.} A deterministic
volume-doubling cycle with identical birth volumes \emph{must} synchronise under uniform growth; real
tissues don't, because cell-cycle times vary. Adding that variability --- a random initial phase at
seed (\texttt{vseed\_cv}) and a Gaussian per-division threshold (\texttt{cycle\_cv}) --- turns the
lockstep wave into a smooth ramp and drops the mid-ramp roughness $0.38\!\to\!0.004$, letting the coral
scale to $500\!\to\!1994$ cells cleanly. One parameter --- but only after the controlled experiment
localised the cause; guessing at shading or T1 tuning would have chased the symptom.
\item \textbf{Honest metrics matter (again).} The first roughness number was inflated by unused
reservoir vertices sitting at the origin. Restricting the metric to \emph{face-referenced} vertices ---
those that actually tile the shell --- gave the true $0.38\!\to\!0.004$ and matched the rendered strip.
Same lesson as the bow-tie faces below: measure the geometric thing you mean.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stage 2 (2026-07-21) --- topology operators, and where the cheap division breaks.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Revert-on-invalid is the right contract for topology.} Rather than reason about
every degenerate case up front, apply the flip/split, validate the touched faces, and roll back if
any is degenerate. T1 then \emph{never} tangles the mesh --- a refused flip is just a no-op. This
turned an error-prone operation into a safe one and is why the self-test is clean.
\item \textbf{Through-vertex division is a $1$--$2$ round operator, not a proliferation engine.}
Splitting a face by a septum between two of its \emph{existing} vertices adds no new vertices, so
each split roughly halves a cell using the same corners; after a couple of rounds cells become
triangles and further splits are refused. Worse, under \emph{uniform growth in a pinned patch} the
target area outruns the available space, every cell is driven below target, and the sliver
daughters collapse --- a run with $231$ forced divisions ended as a red tangle
($79$--$157$ invalid faces). The clean result above deliberately does \emph{one} desynchronised
round with no global growth. The proper fix (tyssue's own) is \emph{edge-midpoint} division:
bisect two opposite edges, insert new vertices (shared with the neighbours across those edges), and
run the septum between them --- well-shaped daughters, sustainable for many rounds. That needs a
vertex buffer with occupancy and neighbour-edge bookkeeping; it is the next increment.
\item \textbf{T1-mediated fluidisation (Stage~3) --- first not sound, then fixed by a \emph{bounded}
Euler step.} The naive attempt failed its own bar: gentle self-propulsion fired $0$ T1s, and the
drive needed to collapse edges made the floppy ($p_0\!=\!4.1$) sheet \emph{diverge} under a
fixed-step relaxation (positions overflow, $\sim\!85$ invalid faces) \emph{before} a T1 could
fluidise it --- the instability was in the \emph{integrator}, not the reconnection (near the
transition the shape energy is nearly flat, so an explicit step lets vertices cross). The sound
fix is a \emph{bounded overdamped Euler} step: cap every per-substep move at $15\%$ of the mean
edge length with \texttt{torch.clamp} --- so no vertex can cross a cell in one step, and the step
stays \emph{differentiable} (no \texttt{scipy} solver; the rollout must remain differentiable for
inverse modelling). With that, the sheet stays stable and T1s fire.
\item \textbf{A second, subtler bug the naked eye caught: bow-tie faces (a false ``valid'').} The
first ``sound'' dichotomy ($23\times$) was \emph{wrong}. The rendered strips showed black star-shaped
gaps where cells overlapped: the T1 flip was producing \emph{self-intersecting} (bow-tie) faces,
and my validity guard --- positive shoelace area, no repeated vertices --- \emph{passed} them,
because a bow-tie has positive shoelace area. So ``$0$ invalid faces'' was a false pass and the
big T1 counts were mostly bad flips. Two fixes: (i) a proper \emph{simple-polygon} test (no two
non-adjacent edges cross) plus a \emph{non-overlap} test on the four faces a flip touches, added to
the accept criterion; and (ii) the flip now \emph{searches} the insertion side (which side of its
anchor the gained vertex goes) $\times$ the mirror sign, and commits only a genuinely simple,
non-overlapping, edge-sharing result. With the honest check the corrected dichotomy is:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
$v_0$ & $l_{\text{th}}$ & solid T1 & fluid T1 & ratio \\
\midrule
0.06 & 0.25 & 96 & 1413 & $\mathbf{14.7\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The fluid still performs $\sim\!15\times$ the T1s of the caged solid (fluid MSD $0.64$ vs solid
$0.10$ --- cage-breaking), now with a \emph{genuinely} valid, non-overlapping tiling at every frame
(the strips tile cleanly). The lesson is the one the whole document keeps re-learning: a numeric
invariant (area $>0$) is not the same as the geometric one you meant (a simple, embedded tiling) ---
and a rendered movie is a sharper validity check than a scalar. The corrected numbers are smaller
than the bogus ones, but the physics --- fluid flows, solid jams --- survives.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stage 1 (2026-07-21) --- force balance is one line, and it just works.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item The inner-relaxation-as-velocity trick (\S3) reaches force balance with no stability
tuning: $\Delta t$ is a free label, \texttt{eta}$=0.08$, $20$ inner steps/frame. Contrast the
SPV prototype, whose entire ``noisy pattern'' saga was a \emph{timestep} artefact. Moving the
DOF from cell centres to mesh vertices \emph{and} minimising to residual removed that whole
failure mode.
\item \textbf{The transition sits where the topology puts it.} With a fixed honeycomb (mostly
hexagons) the residual energy already collapses across $p_0\!\approx\!3.72$--$3.81$: the
single-cell floor for a regular hexagon is $p_0^\ast(\text{hex})=3.722$, so $\langle q\rangle$
cannot fall below $\approx\!3.72$ no matter how small $p_0$ is (the \texttt{solid} cases pin
there). The canonical $3.81$ is the \emph{disordered}-polygon value; sharpening the transition
to exactly $3.81$ needs T1 rearrangements to populate the pentagon/heptagon classes --- the next
operator. So the transition is not a fixed number but a readout of the polygon-class
distribution, which is a topology property.
\item \textbf{The engine calls \texttt{forward()} under \texttt{no\_grad}.} The autograd force
needs an explicit \texttt{with torch.enable\_grad()} (as the SPV op also did) --- otherwise
\texttt{torch.autograd.grad} raises ``does not require grad''. Cost one run to rediscover.
\item \textbf{Pin the border or the patch curls.} A free (unpinned) bounded sheet contracts
under perimeter tension and the boundary ring buckles inward, contaminating the bulk statistics.
Pinning vertices of degree $<3$ (those on the ragged Voronoi border) holds the frame; bulk stats
exclude any face touching a pinned vertex.
\end{itemize}

Aggregate), edge-midpoint division with a decidable long-axis angle, and the lift to the closed 3D
monolayer (per-cell volume elasticity $+$ surface T1) all exist and are exercised above; the
reaction--diffusion runs on the cell set and rides division; and the division wave is tamed by a
stochastic cell cycle. What remains are the morphogenesis \emph{targets} from Turing\_vertex, and they
reuse every operator above unchanged --- only the coupling and the fluidity are new dials.
(i)~\emph{Morphogen-driven growth}: \texttt{morphogen\_growth\_3d} grows a cell's target volume where
the activator $a$ is high, so a localised Turing patch on a \emph{fluid} ($p_0\!>\!3.81$) shell, where
T1 lets the tissue flow and its cells divide (handing the activator to their daughters), should
\emph{bud and extend into a tube} rather than jam into a spike (Turing\_vertex Fig.~5). (ii)~Lowering
the reaction--diffusion feed drives that patch to \emph{branch} (Fig.~6). The exploratory \texttt{tube}
runner --- the same schedule with the morphogen$\to$growth coupling switched on and $p_0$ raised into
the fluid regime --- is the current front; stabilising the bud against the fluid shell's tendency to
balloon is the open problem.

\paragraph{The activator floods the shell under growth --- diagnosis (2026-07-23).}
Coupling Gierer--Meinhardt to growth, the activator went from a few clean localised spots to
nearly-all-red in a handful of frames. Watching the frames pinned the cause: it is \emph{not} stochastic
and \emph{not} the peak running away --- it is \textbf{domain-growth pattern insertion}. With no growth
(the rate$=0$ study) GM held three stable spots on a genuinely white background; the flood appears
\emph{only when the tissue grows}, because a Turing-\emph{unstable} RD tiles the enlarging shell with new
structures at its natural wavelength. The visible ``white $\to$ light red'' creep is the uniform basal
source $a_0$ igniting each background cell, which the autocatalysis $a^2/h$ then amplifies. Confinement
levers were tried and each failed a different way: \emph{Meinhardt saturation} bounds the peak but
converts spots into \emph{rings} (activated annulus, white centre --- the classic saturated-GM signature),
\emph{strong inhibition} suppresses the seeds before they establish, and \emph{slow growth} stops the
flood but starves the pattern (almost no activation). The correct route is therefore to remove the
background ignition ($a_0\!\to\!0$) and stay in a \emph{localised-structure/hysteretic} regime where
seeded spots persist and the homogeneous state is stable --- matching Okuda's own explanation that
stability under growth comes from ``the intrinsic hysteresis in the activator--inhibitor system with
domain growth'' at $\gamma\!=\!1$. Fixing this is a prerequisite to any tube; see the gap-analysis
section for the full mechanism map.

\paragraph{Correction: the morphogen must be an AMOUNT, and the flood is a $\gamma$ problem (2026-07-23).}
The saturation/inhibition confinement levers above were the wrong battle, and re-reading Okuda's Appendix~A
says why. The evolved morphogen DOF is the molecule \emph{amount} $m_j$ (conserved within the cell); the
concentration $c_j=m_j/v_j$ appears \emph{only} as the argument of the reaction and flux terms. Our RD
instead stored a \emph{concentration} and \texttt{morphogen\_growth\_3d} inflated $v_j$ \emph{without touching
it} --- so every growth step \emph{silently created mass} (the opposite error to the dilution one), which
\emph{spuriously} helped the peak ride the tip. The fix is structural, not a continuum $-c(\nabla\!\cdot\!v)$
term: conserve amount by rescaling $c_j\!\leftarrow\!c_j\,(v_{\text{old}}/v_{\text{new}})=c_j\,(s_{\text{prev}}/s)^3$
as $v_{\text{eq}}\!\sim\!s^3$ grows (division is already correct --- daughters share the mother's $c$, and
their volumes sum to hers, so amount is conserved). Crucially, dilution is now \emph{load-bearing} and kept,
not cancelled --- Okuda's intra-domain gradients are \emph{caused} by it. With dilution correct, the flood
(``the peak washes out'') is Okuda's $\gamma=0.01$ regime: patterning too slow relative to deformation. The
remedy is a \emph{rate inequality} --- raise $\gamma$ (reaction outruns dilution; his undulation/tubulation
figures use $\gamma=100$), not saturation. The amplification that makes a bud commit lives on the
\emph{mechanics}: growth is a steep Hill \emph{switch} on $c$ ($\alpha\gg1$, $\rho_{\text{sw}}=a_{\text{sw}}$
at the midpoint of the two GM steady states). Next: reproduce Fig.~4a --- fix $\chi$, sweep $\gamma$, and
recover the three regimes (blur / hold / hold-with-spot-increase) to calibrate the timescale dial before any
tube run.

\paragraph{Monolayer-thickness operator built (2026-07-23).}
The C\#1 gap operator (\texttt{monolayer\_design.md}) is implemented as the \texttt{monolayer}
\emph{implementation} of the \texttt{shape\_energy\_3d} contract (plexus2 sec.~5: same biology, different
numerics): every cell gets its own 3D volume $+$ surface by offsetting an apical$/$basal shell $\pm h/2$ along
the \emph{vertex} normal, with Okuda's Eq.~3 energy ($\tfrac12 k_v(v_j-v_{\text{eq}j})^2+\kappa_s s_j$). A
direct physics test (\texttt{test\_monolayer.py}) confirms the geometry ($\sum v_j/(A\,h_0)=0.988$) and,
decisively, the \emph{emergent bending} signature: on the curved shell the apical$/$basal area ratio is
$1.176$ vs.\ the theoretical $((R{+}h/2)/(R{-}h/2))^2=1.174$ --- a $0.2\%$ match, so surface tension penalises
curvature with \emph{no} explicit $K_{\text{bend}}$. A localised $v_{\text{eq}}$ increase buckles the cap
outward. (The rest shell settles smaller than $v_{\text{eq}}$ --- the correct $\kappa_s$/$k_v$ force balance,
a calibration knob.)

\paragraph{The tube, in nine rounds --- an autonomous ledger (2026-07-23).}
Rounds 22--30 worked the three coupled issues (RD stability, tip-riding, interface orientation) as
small hypothesis/validation/falsification steps on the L4 cluster (8 jobs/round), and got from ``the
activator floods'' to ``a $\chi$-diameter tube.''
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{R22 --- amount-conservation \& $\gamma$ (issue 1).} A/B: \emph{off} (conc.\ held while cells
inflate = mass created) floods to red\_frac 0.46 / over\_tip 3.95; \emph{on} halves it (0.33 / 1.54).
Regime: fast growth (low $\gamma$) floods (0.66), slow growth (high $\gamma$) holds (0.13) --- Okuda Fig.~4a.
\item \textbf{R23 --- sweet spot.} Falsified ``fast RD helps'' (rd\_rate$\ge$2 floods-then-collapses, 9k cells).
Winner \texttt{rate=0.010, rd\_rate=1}: over\_tip 1.0, protr 1.14, tip\_act 0.80, 1929 cells --- a confined
tip-riding bud (issues 1+2).
\item \textbf{R24 --- falsified ``longer\,=\,bigger.''} At 350 frames the bud deflates and the activator
\emph{refills the growing domain} (flood peak 0.9): amount-conservation only \emph{delays} the flood.
\item \textbf{R25 --- sustain (issue 1, deeper).} $a_0\!\to\!0$ (body can't ignite) $+$ amount-conservation
keeps the body white the \emph{whole} run: flood peak $0.9\!\to\!0.30$, bud persists, 2300 cells. $a_0$
was round-21's right idea, incomplete without amount-conservation.
\item \textbf{R26 --- falsified mechanical-only interface.} Purse-string $+$ extrusion shape the bud but
build \emph{no wall} (tube\_len 0): the wall needs \emph{cells}, placed by oriented division.
\item \textbf{R27 --- oriented division (issue 3).} \texttt{divide\_3d orient\_iface}: a red cell's daughters
stack \emph{along the bud axis} (normal to the body). Alone still a nub --- the patch is sub-$\chi$ and
in-plane division spreads laterally; extension needs a strong outward push.
\item \textbf{R28 --- TUBES.} Strong extrusion (K\_extrude$\ge$4) $+$ oriented division $+$ a $\chi$-sized
spot $\Rightarrow$ \texttt{tube\_len}$\approx$2, \texttt{n\_tubes}=2, red at the neck, confined (over\_tip
$\approx$1). Diameter tracks $\chi$ (chi4$\to$1.9, chi8$\to$3.3).
\item \textbf{R29 --- falsified ``elongate by running longer.''} At 450--550 frames the tube is \emph{lost}
(transient peaking $\sim$frame 350); the purse-string neck did not hold it.
\item \textbf{R30 --- $\chi\to$diameter law (capstone).} Sweeping $\chi$ at 350 frames, tube diameter rises
\emph{monotonically} ($\chi$2$\to$1.06 \dots $\chi$8$\to$3.31); above $\chi\!\sim\!8$ it saturates and
fragments into multiple tubes. Okuda's \emph{qualitative} law ($\chi$ sets the diameter) is reproduced;
the exponent ($\sim$0.8 in the clean regime, $\sim$0.3 over the full range) only approximates his $1/4$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Net.} All three issues are mechanistically solved and combine into a $\chi$-diameter budding tube.
Open: the tube is a \emph{transient} (degrades past $\sim$frame 350 as the domain grows) and a \emph{lobe}
more than a long cylinder --- sustained elongation (a moving growth zone that truly rides the tip without
letting the body domain enlarge) is the next front.

\section{BREAKTHROUGH: a working tube (rounds 31--34, 2026-07-23)}
Rounds 22--30 solved the three coupled issues and produced a $\chi$-diameter \emph{budding lobe}, but it was
a transient that \emph{capped} (protr $\sim$1.4) and could not elongate. Rounds 31--34 broke through to a
\textbf{genuine tube} --- long, thin, constant-diameter --- and with it the character of the work changes:
the \emph{emergence} question (can this pipeline make a tube at all?) is answered \textbf{yes}; from here it is
\emph{optimisation} of a working phenomenon, not a search for it.

\paragraph{The four steps to the breakthrough.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{R31 --- a smaller spot gives a thinner, longer finger.} Shrinking the activated patch
(\texttt{cone\_deg}\,8, \texttt{chi}\,4) turned the fat lobe (len/diam $\sim$0.8) into a \emph{thin finger}
(\texttt{c8}: len/diam $=1.99$; \texttt{c10\_chi3}: $1.95$), super-confined (redpk 0.05--0.09) with less
proliferation. Less activated material $\Rightarrow$ slower domain growth $\Rightarrow$ later flood.
\item \textbf{R32 --- more frames don't elongate it (falsified).} At $2\times$ frames the tube \emph{caps}
(protr peaks $\sim$1.4 mid-run then degrades) --- because the activation is a \emph{widening patch}, not a
moving tip. Only \texttt{grow\_after}\,150 (settle longer before growth) sustained it, and still as a lobe.
\item \textbf{R33 --- TIP-TRACKING: the breakthrough.} The activation must be a fixed-\emph{size} cap that
\emph{rides the advancing tip} (a fixed-\emph{angle} cone widens with radius $\to$ fat lobe). New seed mode
\texttt{tip}: each frame re-activate a cap of radius \texttt{tip\_radius} at the current outermost cell, so
it follows the tip $\to$ \emph{constant-diameter extension}. Result: real long tubes ---
\texttt{tr25} len/diam $=3.0$, \texttt{tr15\_r015} $=3.4$, \texttt{tr15\_long} (700 frames) len $9.4$,
\textbf{len/diam $=4.2$} (a genuine thin tube, not a lobe).
\item \textbf{R34 --- shaping \& viewing.} Thin (\texttt{tip\_radius}\,1.2), strong extrusion to round and push
the flat tip outward, the spot seeded on the side so the tube grows in \emph{profile}, and a cross-section
taken \emph{in the plane of the tubing} (contains the tube axis, coloured by activator) so the red protuberance
reads correctly against the 3D view.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The working recipe (honest scope).} A tube forms from: (i) the emergent-confinement machinery of
R22--30 --- amount-conservation ($c=m/v$, growth dilutes), $a_0\!\to\!0$ (body stays white), the rate sweet
spot, oriented interface division (daughters stack along the bud axis $=$ normal to the body); \emph{plus}
(ii) a \textbf{tip-tracking activation driver} that supplies the sustained, constant-size apical signal the
emergent RD alone could not maintain (the RD held a confined bud but capped). So the tube is not \emph{fully}
emergent --- the tip signal is driven --- but every mechanical ingredient (confinement, wall-building
division, extrusion) is, and the driver is the minimal honest stand-in for ``the activator peak stays at the
apex.'' Diameter is set by \texttt{tip\_radius} ($=$ Okuda's $\chi$ role); length by frames $\times$ growth.

\paragraph{From here: optimisation, not emergence.} The open directions are now tuning, not existence:
thinner/longer tubes (\texttt{tip\_radius}, growth, frames), a rounded non-flat tip (tip pressure), multiple
tubes (Fig~5 starfish), branching (Fig~6, low $\gamma$), and --- separately --- \emph{computation} throughput
(profile the first 200 frames, vectorise/\texttt{torch.compile} the hot loops, keep rendering off the critical
path) so more of the phenomenon space can be swept per hour.

\section{Differentiable mechanics analysis (\texttt{analyze\_forces.py})}
A standalone tool re-runs a run and quantifies, per cell and per frame, the mechanical fields --- built to be
\textbf{torch-differentiable} so a downstream loss (expel the tip, relieve tube stress) can be optimised.
The vertex-model energy is
\begin{equation}
U=\sum_{j\in\text{cells}}\Big[K_A(A_j-A_j^0)^2+K_P(P_j-P_j^0)^2+\tfrac12\Gamma P_j^2+K_V(v_j-v_j^{\text{eq}})^2\Big]
  \;+\;\Lambda\!\!\sum_{e\in\text{edges}}\!\ell_e,
\end{equation}
with per-cell area $A_j$, perimeter $P_j$, volume $v_j$ (their targets $A_j^0,P_j^0,v_j^{\text{eq}}$ recorded
each frame) and edge lengths $\ell_e$. The quantities plotted are:
\begin{align}
\textbf{Force (per vertex):}\quad & \mathbf{F}_i=-\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial \mathbf{x}_i}
  \quad\text{(one autograd pass; per-cell value} = \text{mean } \|\mathbf{F}_i\|\text{ over the cell's ring).}\\
\textbf{Stress / pressure (per cell):}\quad & p_j=-\frac{\partial U}{\partial v_j}=2K_V\,(v_j^{\text{eq}}-v_j)
  \quad\text{(}{>}0:\text{ below target volume }\Rightarrow\text{ pushes outward).}\\
\textbf{Cortical tension (per cell):}\quad & T_j=\frac{\partial U}{\partial P_j}=2K_P(P_j-P_j^0)+\Gamma P_j+\Lambda .
\end{align}
Movement is the centroid nearest-neighbour displacement; divisions are cells appended since the previous
frame; tracking follows cells sampled at the final frame \emph{backward} by nearest centroid (so tube cells,
born late, are included). \emph{A note on provenance:} Okuda's paper is written entirely at the level of
\emph{energy and force balance} (Eq.~1 is $\eta(\dot r-V_f)=-\nabla U$; ``cells generate mechanical forces'')
--- it never computes a \emph{stress or pressure field} (``stress'' appears once, generically; ``pressure''
and ``tension'' never). Our per-cell pressure is derived \emph{from his own energy}, $p=-\partial U/\partial
v=2K_V(v^{\text{eq}}-v)$ (the Eq.~3 volume-elasticity term), so it is fully consistent with his mechanics ---
but \emph{visualising the stress/pressure distribution is a diagnostic lens Okuda did not apply}. That extra
lens is precisely what made the hollow defect legible below; the energy-only view would not have. Output: \texttt{quantification.npz} (per-frame per-cell tables) $+$ a 2$\times$2
\texttt{analysis.mp4} (force $\mid$ stress $\mid$ division $\mid$ tracking). On the long tube (aspect $10.7$)
the tool shows the \emph{tip is the force/pressure hot-spot} --- the tube is under high stress, the concrete
target for the next optimisation.

\section{Reading the mechanics: the hollow problem is explosive, decoupled growth (2026-07-23)}
Running \texttt{analyze\_forces.py} on the current best tube (\texttt{round\_37\_kv6}: aspect $\sim$10, cell
CV crushed to $0.97$ by stiffening $K_V$ to $6$, the earlier CV goal essentially met) turned the persistent
\emph{hollow} defect ($>300$ hollow cells, systematically, in every run's \texttt{metrics.png}) from a
symptom into a diagnosis. The four panels agree:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Force} $\|\!-\!\nabla U\|$ is concentrated \emph{entirely in the tube} (wall + neck bright); the
body is force-free. The tube is the only load-bearing structure --- the body is mechanically idle.
\item \textbf{Stress / pressure} $p_j=2K_V(v_j^{\text{eq}}-v_j)$ is strongly \emph{positive and localised to
the tube} (red), the body relaxed (white). Positive $p$ means the tube cells are \emph{below} target volume
and straining outward: they are compressed by the extrusion and want to expand --- an over-pressurised state.
\item \textbf{Migration} (velocity-thresholded) is almost all at the \emph{tube tip} (cells being extruded),
with the body essentially static. The deformation does \emph{not} propagate to the body: the tube is
mechanically \emph{decoupled}.
\item \textbf{Division} (births) is localised at the same \emph{tip} --- proliferation coincides exactly with
the force/pressure/migration hot-spot.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Diagnosis.} Everything piles up at the tip --- pressure, births, migration --- and nothing transmits
to the body. The growth is \emph{explosive and localised}: tip cells inflate and divide faster than the T1
network can rearrange them, so they buckle rather than flow into an ordered wall $\Rightarrow$ the hollow
cells. The pressure being trapped in the tube (not shared with the body) is the mechanical signature of an
\emph{unsustainable} growth rate.

\textbf{Hypothesis (the new perspective, from the mechanics).} Grow \emph{slower}, so that per unit growth
there is more T1 flow: cells arrange along the tube, the load transmits tube$\rightarrow$body, the tube
pressure relaxes, and the hollow buckling disappears. Slower-but-longer runs keep the tube length. This
reframes the tube as a \emph{quasi-static} morphogenesis (Okuda's regime) rather than a fast extrusion, and
makes the differentiable stress field (above) the natural objective: minimise tube pressure / equalise it
tube$\leftrightarrow$body. \texttt{round\_39} tests exactly this (rate $0.006\!\to\!0.004$, longer runs, more
aggressive T1). The optimisation shifts from ``can we make a tube?'' (solved) through ``clean cells''
(CV solved by $K_V$) to ``a \emph{sustainable}, low-stress tube'' --- read directly off the force/pressure
maps, not guessed.

\paragraph{The mature picture.} The morphogenesis is now legible as a coupled \emph{geometry--force--pressure
dynamics}: the cell geometry sets the energy, the energy gradient is the force, the volume deficit is the
pressure, and their interplay (mediated by T1 rearrangement and oriented division) drives the shape. Read
this way, a \emph{good} morphogenesis is one whose dynamics are \textbf{sustainable} (bounded stress,
tube$\leftrightarrow$body load sharing, no buckling), \textbf{repeatable} (the same rule makes the same tube
across seeds and scales), and \textbf{emerging} (the tube is produced by the local mechanics, not imposed).
The differentiable force/pressure field makes each of these a measurable objective rather than an aesthetic
judgement --- which is the point of building the analysis before pushing the phenomenon further.

\paragraph{Okuda's own answer: the process is QUASI-STATIC (2026-07-23).} Re-reading the paper for the
hollow/stress problem gives the mechanism directly. Okuda writes: \emph{``By assuming a quasi-static process,
the cell cycle $\tau_{\text{cycle}}$ was set to be much larger than the characteristic time of cell
deformation, represented by $\eta_c/\kappa_s$''}, and relaxes each configuration until \emph{``the mean
residual error is under the threshold $E_{\text{th}}$''} before the next event. In words: after every growth
or division increment the tissue \emph{fully relaxes to force balance} (and reconnects, edge $<\Delta l_{\text{th}}$)
\emph{before} anything else changes. That is precisely the ``slower growth so cells rearrange'' hypothesis,
made exact --- and it is exactly what our runs violate: a \emph{fixed} \texttt{relax\_iters} per frame with a
fast tip-tracking extrusion drives growth faster than the mesh can relax, so the tube is built out of
never-equilibrated cells (pressure $\sim$3.1, fresh tiny daughters, curvature). The fix is therefore not a
parameter nudge but a \emph{regime}: relax to a residual threshold (not a fixed iteration count) and keep
$\tau_{\text{cycle}}\gg\eta_c/\kappa_s$. Two corollaries from the same reading: the constant tube diameter
comes from the \emph{maintained activator-spot size} (our fixed-radius tip cap already does this), and
division is \emph{normal to the cell's own longest axis} --- in a quasi-static tube the wall cells elongate
along the tube, so a plain Hertwig long-axis split already extends it (the forced bud-axis orientation may be
unnecessary once the dynamics are quasi-static). \texttt{round\_41+} pursue this: relax-to-residual, slow
cycle, Hertwig division.
