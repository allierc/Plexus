\section{Mechanism}

Every model in \code{connectome-gnn} is a one-step message-passing network on a \emph{given}
connectome. Two things wanted now sit outside that: a deeper, edge-stateful processor
(GraphCast's), and datasets with no connectome at all. ZAPBench has $71{,}721$ neurons of which
about $481$ are matched to an electron-microscopy reconstruction; the redox organoid is a
three-dimensional field with no segmentation. In both, the coupling has to come from \emph{space}
rather than from wiring.

The arithmetic that decides what can be represented: a ZAPBench volume is acquired plane by plane,
$72$ planes at $12.0$\,ms, so one volume takes $0.851$\,s against a $0.914$\,s frame interval ---
$93\%$ of the frame period. Neurons at different depths are therefore \emph{not} sampled
simultaneously, which is precisely what a message-passing operator assumes. That fact is data, not
a modelling choice: the per-plane acquisition times are recorded, and the model is conditioned on
the offset rather than pretending it away.

\section{Equations and symbols}

\emph{To be written at stage 2, when the operator exists.} The two message forms this prototype
selects between are
\begin{align}
\text{\code{simple}}\quad & \mathbf{m}_i = \sum_{j} W_{ij}\, g_\phi(v_j, a_j) ,
\qquad \dot v_i = f_\theta(v_i, a_i, \mathbf{m}_i, b_i s(t))
\label{eq:simple}\\[4pt]
\text{\code{graphcast}}\quad & e_{ij} \leftarrow e_{ij} + \mathrm{LN}\big(\mathrm{MLP}_e([e_{ij}\|v_j\|v_i])\big),
\qquad v_i \leftarrow v_i + \mathrm{LN}\big(\mathrm{MLP}_v([v_i\|\textstyle\sum_j e_{ij}])\big)
\label{eq:graphcast}
\end{align}
with $a_i$ the per-neuron embedding carrying heterogeneity invisible in the state, and $b_i$ the
per-neuron gain on the external stimulus $s(t)$.

\section{The Plexus2 container}

\emph{To be written as the operators land.} Sets: \code{neuron}. Fields: \code{stim} (playback),
\code{mesh} (only when the encoder/decoder option is on). The four architectural choices are
\emph{config options}, not forks --- \code{encoder\_decoder}, \code{message}, \code{n\_passes},
\code{embedding} --- which is why the gate table below counts $24$ combinations.

\section{Gates}

A gate is a number with a threshold decided \emph{before} the run and a stated consequence if it
fails. A threshold chosen after seeing the number is not a threshold, so every threshold below is
a literal in \code{gates.py} and predates the code it gates. Three tiers, and only the third is a
statement about biology; this table is \tierProportion, so two thirds of it is verification. That
is the expected shape and is stated rather than hidden.

A gate's threshold belongs in the unit of the phenomenon, not of the mesh --- a threshold in grid
cells is denominated in the currency in which it is easiest to pass, and a green row of that kind
reads as an endorsement the interface has not earned. Every row therefore carries what its number
is \emph{of}, and the measurement tier is only available because the spec declares
\code{general.units:}.

\section{The evidence}

Every gate above points to a figure, and a gate with no artifact is reported as \gskip{SKIP}
rather than passed. That is not bookkeeping for its own sake: three defects in the toy were found
this way and each was a number that looked plausible --- a circuit sitting at a fixed point
(spatial spread $9.29$ against a temporal spread of $0.065$), a stimulus field that was
\emph{identically zero} for three generations because \op{activation\_pulse} reads a clock no
operator was writing, and a spatial type purity of $6.1\times$ chance that was the grid being finer
than the sampling. All three are obvious in a panel and invisible in a scalar.
