\section{What we are doing --- two things at once}

\textbf{Track A --- build the method.} An automated loop whose unit of discovery is a
\emph{mechanism}: it proposes a change to the model's \textbf{structure} --- remove the pushing
force, add oriented cell division, wire the growth gate to the reaction instead of the seed ---
writes down what it expects \emph{before} running, runs the simulation, measures, and records
whether it was right.

\textbf{Parameters are in scope; they are just not discoveries.} ``What does this mechanism do as
you turn it up'' is the second question Track~A asks of every mechanism, and a sweep is a
legitimate experiment. What must never happen is a retune being reported as a new idea --- and that
is now enforced by arithmetic rather than by prohibition: \code{comp\_hash} excludes parameters, so
a composition at a different operating point has the \emph{same identity} and is filed as a point
on a known mechanism. The rule holds because the identity function says so, not because an agent
was asked to be careful.

\textbf{And it must be allowed to be curious.} A loop that may only test predictions will only ever
propose things it can predict, which is how a campaign came to spend nineteen of thirty-four
predictions on ``nothing will happen'' and count eighteen confirmations that a sphere stayed a
sphere. An exploratory slot states what it is \emph{varying} and what it will \emph{report}, rather
than what it expects. Turning $\chi$ up and describing what appears is an experiment; a labyrinth
nobody forecast is a finding, not a failed prediction.

\textbf{Track B --- reproduce Okuda's result, as the proof that the method is worth having.} Okuda
et al.\ (2018) grew a hollow ball of cells that spontaneously forms tubes, undulations and branches,
driven by a chemical pattern on its own surface. We are rebuilding that here, and \emph{the figure
is not a side quest --- it is the evidence.}

\textbf{What we may honestly claim, and what we may not.} The composition carrying Okuda's coupling
is \textbf{supplied} to the search, as a starting point on the frontier --- it is written down in
\code{reference\_recipes()} and the loop begins from it. So the claim is \emph{not} ``the loop found
the mechanism by itself''. What the loop does, and what would be worth publishing, is the
\textbf{lever map around that mechanism}: which operators are necessary, which are inert, what
happens at the extremes, and which of the four morphologies each choice reaches --- every one of
those a prediction made before the run and scored after it. If a composition the loop reaches by
its own edits ever beats the supplied one, that is a stronger claim and it will be stated
separately. Until then the honest sentence is: \emph{the mechanism was given; the map around it was
earned.}

The two tracks feed each other: the reproduction is the honest test of the method, and the method
is what makes the reproduction more than hand-tuning.

\section{What we believe about the biology}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=grey!6,colframe=grey!45,boxrule=0.7pt,arc=2pt]
\begin{center}\large\textbf{Nothing \emph{measured}. But we now write down what we assume.}\end{center}

The register of \emph{results} is still empty, and should stay empty until a batch has been taken
with instruments that work. Every number we have was measured with at least one broken ruler in the
path.

What changed on 31 July is a different thing: the register of \textbf{assumptions} is no longer
empty. Eleven basics about cell tissue are written down in \texttt{discovery\_okuda/PREMISES.md} --- not a
literature review, but the minimum a person needs in order to look at one of our runs and say
``that cannot be right''. Each is phrased so that a computer can check it against a run, and each
one now does (Phase~1).

The distinction matters. A result is something we found out. A premise is something we are
\emph{assuming}, stated plainly enough that it can be caught being wrong. Writing the second kind
down is not a claim about biology --- it is a way of making our own mistakes visible.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{The team of agents}\label{sec:agents}

\textbf{\code{ROLES.md} is the source of truth, and the code is checked against it in both
directions} by \code{roles.py -{}-check} --- the same discipline \code{PREMISES.md} has for the
biology. The roster had reached sixteen roles nobody could account for, because it lived in four
places at once: the code, a table in this note, a figure drawn by hand, and everybody's memory.
Four descriptions of one thing are three descriptions that are wrong. There is now one, and this
note does not repeat it.

\textbf{The roster was judged by a ledger, not by argument.} A live round spent 23.5 minutes across
sixteen calls, of which the Proposer took 14.8 --- 63\% of all agent time --- while the Judge and
the Referee were \emph{never called at all}. That is not a claim that they were redundant; it is a
record showing they had nothing to do. The cause was a borrowed design: \emph{Co-Scientist} runs a
tournament of hypotheses because it performs no experiments, so its proposals can only be debated.
We measure. Where a tournament would rank two ideas by argument, we run both.

\textbf{Three acts, and the division that matters.} Act~1 proposes, Act~2 measures, Act~3 decides.
Within that, the load-bearing split is between what is \emph{arithmetic} and what is
\emph{judgement}: the Critic decides whether a batch \textbf{can} be run and its refusals are
enumerable and arguable-with-never; peer-review decides whether it is \textbf{worth} running, which
is a judgement, and it advises rather than vetoes. A judgement that can silently cost twelve
GPU-hours is a veto wearing an adviser's name.

\textbf{A round that produces no evidence is aborted} and routes back to Act~1 carrying the
refusals. It is recorded in full but does not increment the round counter and enters no coverage
denominator --- counting it as a normal round is exactly how a log came to tell the Proposer
\code{coverage 0\%}, from which it correctly concluded its own ledger was broken. Two consecutive
aborts stop the campaign and ask, because a second abort means the refusal reasons were not
actionable, which is a fact about us rather than about the search.

\textbf{As of Phase~8 the loop can be run with no agent in it at all} (\S8.1), and every hand-off
between these roles is declared in \code{WIRING.md} and checked (\S8.2). Those two are what make
the paragraphs above testable rather than remembered.

\section{The phases}

Eight stages. \textbf{I stop at the end of each one and wait for you.} That is the partition.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 0 --- Pre-flight: fix the instruments \hfill \dn\ (28 of 28)}
\textbf{Goal.} Make every measurement trustworthy. Nothing runs unattended until this lands. Not one
of the twenty-eight items is science; each one makes a question \emph{askable}.

\textbf{The last two, closed.} \emph{Comparing a batch at a common frame} --- the per-run evidence
horizon was right and did not make a BATCH comparable. If one run tears at frame 300 and another
holds to 700, comparing their ``final'' states puts a torn mesh beside a healthy one and reports
the difference as biology: both operands valid, the comparison wrong, which is the shape of every
error that has cost this campaign a batch. A batch is now read at one frame, the earliest horizon

references over in one step leaves a window where a key is written by half the code and read by the

that quietly learns nothing.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 1 --- Teach the loop what a tissue is: the premise gate \hfill \dn}
\textbf{Why this became its own phase.} Ten defects were found in one day, every one by Cedric
looking at a picture, and all of the same kind: an operator or an instrument not doing what its name
says. The loop checked that runs finished, that the mesh was valid, that the numbers were finite. It
never once checked that the thing it had simulated was a tissue.

\textbf{Eleven basics, each one something a computer can run and fail}, in three tiers by cost: read
the recipe (instant, refuses a hopeless run before a GPU is touched); read the run (free, goes
loudly into the record); and run a small experiment of its own --- switch everything off and let the
ball sit for forty frames; it must not change size.

\textbf{A broken premise must be declared, not silenced.} Switching off growth to ask what a
protrusion is made of genuinely breaks premise~1, and that is science. So a recipe may waive a
premise \emph{in writing, with a reason}; an undeclared violation is a hard stop. That is the only
thing separating ``we tested an idea'' from ``the settings were wrong''.

\textbf{Every check was proved to fail before being trusted.} The one to look at: run against the
code as it stood that morning, a resting ball collapses \textbf{5.00 $\to$ 0.69} in forty frames.
That single check, which costs about a minute, would have caught the worst defect of the campaign on
day one.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 3 --- Switch on the agents that never run \hfill \dn\ (3 of 3)}
\textbf{Ablation is compulsory.} A causal claim needs two experiments: adding a mechanism and
seeing the shape appear shows it is \emph{sufficient}; taking it away and seeing the shape die
shows it is \emph{necessary}. Either alone is an opinion. Every claim now declares its kind and the
checker throws out a batch that asks for more than it tests, \emph{before} any simulation starts.
Sufficient earns ``is sufficient for''; both earn ``causes''; neither earns ``is associated with''.
\textbf{The word can no longer outrun the experiment.}

\textbf{The Grounder is wired.} The only agent that reads the paper had no call site anywhere ---
every entry point was exercised by its own smoke test alone. Batch construction now asks it, and it
answers with Okuda's numbers carrying the sentence they came from. They arrive as \emph{parameters},
so composition identity is unchanged: setting the starting size is a retune, not a new mechanism.
It \emph{advises} --- the faithful share of a batch inherits his numbers, the exploratory share is
free to leave them, because a search that may only stand where the paper stands cannot discover
that the paper is not the only place to stand.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 4 --- Measurements that can tell his regimes apart \hfill \dn}
\textbf{The classifier.} Sphere, undulation, tube, branched, plus \textbf{invalid} (the surface
passes through itself, so no shape reading means anything) and \textbf{unclear}, returned
deliberately near a boundary. A classifier that always answers can never be wrong, and one that can
never be wrong teaches nothing. ``Invalid'' beats every morphology --- this campaign has already
once reported a crumpled shell as a result. Until 1~August nothing called it: built, certified,
never asked.

\textbf{The calibration, and the surprise.} The plan was to tune until Okuda's handful of spots
appeared on a 2000-cell ball, then freeze. Counting spots over time --- for the first time ---
showed the pattern never settles: 104 spots at step~0, 19 at 100, 7 at 200, and \textbf{1 by step
400}, where it stays. It keeps merging. So ``the right number of spots'' is not a setting to be
found but \textbf{a moment to be chosen}, and the choice has to be stated rather than tuned into.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 4b --- Can the chemistry be simulated at all? \hfill \dn}
\textbf{Why this became a phase mid-flight.} The pre-launch stopped on a run whose chemistry turned
to infinities within a hundred frames. The rule that exists to prevent exactly this had been
\textbf{fifty times too generous} for as long as the campaign has existed.

\textbf{How.} The stability condition was written down as a \emph{comment}, worked out once, for the
settings of the day. Then the defaults changed, and Phase~2 deliberately widened the ranges so
Okuda's own values would be reachable --- and the arithmetic in the comment quietly stopped
describing the code. On top of that it counted one factor twice, reporting a fiftieth of the true
figure. \textbf{A comment cannot be re-derived; it can only be re-read, and nobody had cause to
re-read it.} The condition is now computed from the settings actually in force, and a composition
that breaches it is refused before it reaches a GPU rather than after.
\end{phasebox}

give within-run replication for free, and it is what Okuda shows. Every control I built during
the pre-launch used one spot or none.

\textbf{Two findings, one of them a problem.} Seeding more widely does not give more pattern --- it
gives \emph{less}, and past 0.15 it gives none at all: a seed covering a third of the shell leaves
no room for the inhibitor to separate domains, so the whole field decays together. The useful band
is narrow and sits below 0.15.

\textbf{And the three seeds were bit-identical} --- 0.501, 0.501, 0.501. \code{general.seed} was
written into every spec and read by \emph{nothing}: both stochastic operators take a \code{seed}
(the mesh jitter, and which cells are nucleated) and the translator passed neither. ``Three
independent runs'' were one run repeated, so every spread this campaign has quoted across seeds
described the floating-point reproducibility of a single trajectory.

\textbf{Fixed, and the fix follows the working specs rather than my first idea.} I offset the two
operators' seeds, reasoning that jitter and nucleation are independent draws. True, and beside the
point: \code{archive\_rounded.py}, \code{archive\_vh\_rd\_coral.py} and \code{run\_tyssue\_fig5.py}
all pass \emph{one shared} SEED to both, and a composition that reproduces an archived run has to
draw the numbers that run drew. Departing from the convention would have made every reproduction
check compare two different experiments. A spec that declares a seed no operator consumes is now
refused outright.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 5 --- The first live run: the agents do not talk to each other \hfill \done}
\textbf{It ran, and that is the point.} Two rounds, eight simulations, 23 agent calls --- and
\textbf{one usable run, map coverage 0\%}. The machinery worked; the science did not, and the
reason was the same everywhere.

\textbf{Not one finding reached another agent.} The Critic refused eleven slots and the Proposer
was never told why. The Biologist judged every specimen and spoke to nobody. An audit of every
hand-off found the same shape \textbf{seven more times}: a role produced a correct judgement, wrote
it down, and no one read it. \emph{The campaign's defining failure was not bad judgement --- it was
correct judgement with no recipient.} Drawing the graph is what made it obvious.

\textbf{Two consequences that are now permanent.} Refusals became evidence: a slot the Critic
rejects is written to \code{batch\_refusals.jsonl} and lands in the next Proposer's prompt, because
a loop told only about its successes cannot avoid repeating its failures. And the Biologist reads
\code{PREMISES.md} mechanically, so the premise gate is a document a person can argue with rather
than a rule buried in code.

\textbf{The black movie}, which is the phase in miniature: frame~0 was perfectly good and was drawn
black because frame~800 diverged --- the colour scale was normalised over the whole run, so one bad
frame erased eight hundred readable ones. Nothing was wrong with the simulation or the plotter
alone; they disagreed about what the scale was for.

\textbf{Closed by Phase 8}, which is what this phase was asking for without being able to name it.
\code{WIRING.md} declares every hand-off and \code{wiring.py} fails the round when an artifact is
written and never read --- the seven-times-repeated shape becomes a failing check rather than an
audit somebody has to remember to run.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 6 --- The rebuilt loop, and its first launch \hfill \done}
\textbf{Goal.} Rebuild the roster around measurement rather than debate, and launch it.

\subsection*{What the cold start found, and what it cost to misread it}
Across all 63 finished runs, \code{n\_protruding} was zero everywhere. \textbf{This project has
never produced a protrusion.} The loop reported that correctly --- and then gave up, which was the
expensive mistake, and it was ours. \textbf{Track~A asks a different question}: a composition that
patterns without protruding is a \emph{point on the map}, not a failure. On the corrected ranking,
\textbf{46 of 63 runs serve Track~A}. Only Track~B can run out of things worth running; the
operator landscape always has more of itself to understand.

\subsection*{Closing Phase 6 --- what was run}
Nine rounds, 2~August 2026. \textbf{79 hypotheses posed, 58 admitted as evidence, 21 refused.} Ten
defects found and fixed while it ran --- among them: the reading loop recorded \emph{one} run per
round and that run was a chimera, assembled from different runs' fields; and the ranking read
\code{premises\_broken} from the wrong dictionary, so it never once saw a broken premise.

\subsection*{Results, and what we conclude}
\textbf{The machinery works.} 30 predictions were refuted and 23 confirmed --- a surprise rate that
means the predictions were real rather than safe. \textbf{Tubes appeared, on sound specimens.}

\textbf{But the ceiling was set in round 3 and never moved.} No valid specimen exceeded
\code{protr\_peak} 1.295 or two tubes; every run above two tubes was invalid --- dead chemistry or a
self-intersecting sheet. And the valid frontier contains \textbf{no reaction--diffusion at all}:
\code{cell\_rd\_seed} and \code{morphogen\_growth\_3d}, but neither \code{cell\_diffuse} nor

\subsection*{The main conclusion: the logic did not emerge}
\textbf{It was not in the agentic loop, and it was never going to arrive by itself.} Nine rounds of
capable agents produced conclusions no evidence supported --- ``the ceiling is FUNDAMENTAL'',
``division is NECESSARY'' --- because nothing in the loop could tell an untested possibility from a
refuted one, and \emph{could be} had nowhere to live. A campaign that cannot represent ``not yet
tried'' will write ``cannot be'' instead. That is what Phase~7 exists to fix, and it is a
\emph{structural} fix: the unearned conclusion has to be unwritable, not discouraged.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 7 --- The agentic loop update: giving the loop a logic \hfill \done}
\textbf{Goal.} Supply the reasoning discipline Phase~6 proved will not emerge on its own --- as
\emph{structure and code}, not as another voice asking the others to be careful. No new agent.

\textbf{Why one phase and not two.} \emph{Absence of evidence read as evidence of absence} and
\emph{absence of an instrument read as absence of an effect} are the same sentence with a different
subject. Both are cured by the same move: make the unearned conclusion \textbf{unwritable}, and
give the loop somewhere to put what it has not yet tried.

\textbf{\code{LOGIC.md}, and the checker that makes it real.} A third source of truth beside the
two that already hold --- \code{ROLES.md} kept honest by \code{roles.py -{}-check},
\code{PREMISES.md} by the Biologist. \code{logic.py -{}-check} \textbf{refuses a malformed claim at
the moment the Meta-review writes it}, so a bad claim never enters the document the Proposer reads.
A rule agents are merely \emph{asked} to honour is the failure mode being removed.

\textbf{Four modalities, and a home for \emph{could be}} --- which was previously unrepresentable,
so a campaign that could not say ``not yet tried'' wrote ``cannot be'' instead.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{6.2cm}@{}}
\textbf{can be} & demonstrated positive & one instance suffices \\
\textbf{cannot be} & universal negative & must carry its quantifier \\
\textbf{cannot not be} & necessity & the most expensive claim of all \\
\textbf{could be} & untested, open & \textbf{free --- and today unrepresentable} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Independence is a distinct condition-signature, not a run count.} Ten GM-with-division runs
collapse to \emph{one} observation. A negative needs three; \textbf{a necessity claim needs three
and a failed rescue attempt} --- a different experiment, not a re-reading of the same one.

\textbf{The ablation asymmetry}, which is Cedric's and is the sharpest idea in the phase: the same
experiment supports a positive strongly and a negative weakly. A null ablation must repeat on an
independent background; a positive passes on one.

\textbf{No conclusion about an unmeasured property.} \code{morphology=sphere} was recorded for the
run carrying the finest Turing pattern in the campaign, because the shape was measured and the
pattern was not. The honest record is \emph{not measured}, and the honest next step is a request
for an instrument --- which the Metrologist now builds \textbf{on demand rather than on schedule},
so it costs nothing in the rounds that need nothing.

\subsection*{Closing Phase 7}

\subsection*{1. Settled by construction: the loop runs with no agent in it}
Every defect above was found by \emph{launching}, which is the most expensive instrument available:
a round costs ten agent-minutes and half an hour of cluster before it will tell you that a display
name was read as an identifier. \textbf{The loop must be testable without an agent and without a
cluster.} One seam --- \code{run\_agent} --- carries every model call, and one --- \code{cluster}
--- carries every job, so both can be substituted for deterministic stubs that return
schema-valid answers, including the \emph{wrong} answers we have actually seen: an edit that names
a phenotype, a verdict with no flag, a caption with no JSON, a proposal under the wrong key.

A full three-act round then runs offline, in seconds, for nothing, and the questions it answers are
exactly the ones that have cost us rounds: does every declared artifact get written, does every
gate fire, does the batch fill, is the record complete before its readers run. A regression becomes
a failing test rather than a wasted evening. \textbf{This is built first, because it is what makes
the rest of the phase checkable.}

\subsection*{2. The entities, as classes and a registry}
\code{ROLES.md} already declares who exists and \code{roles.py --check} keeps it honest --- but it
checks \emph{existence only}. Each role also declares \textbf{Sends to:}, and nothing has ever
verified that those fifteen edges correspond to a read anywhere in the code. So a \code{Role} and
an \code{Artifact} become objects in a registry, each artifact naming its writer, its readers and
its schema, and the checker fails the round when:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item an artifact is written and never read --- five exist today: \code{causal\_descriptions.md},
      \code{diagnoses.jsonl}, \code{archivist.jsonl}, \code{holes.jsonl}, \code{logic\_report.jsonl}
\item a declared \textbf{Sends to:} edge has no corresponding read
\item a prompt asserts a guarantee no checker enforces
\item a rendering is assigned to an identifier field
\end{itemize}

\noindent This is the same idiom a fourth time --- a document that is the source of truth beside a
checker that enforces it --- applied to the one thing currently specified only in comments.

\begin{resultbox}{8.2 --- Built \hfill \done}
\code{WIRING.md} declares every artifact, its writer and its readers; \code{wiring.py} derives the
same graph from the code and reports the difference. \textbf{The rule that carries the weight needs

is the rule that was broken five times, and it caught \code{batch\_attrition.jsonl} --- which I had
added an hour earlier and nothing read --- within the hour.

module-constant aliases --- took the false alarms from about twenty-five to none, because

the declaration decides; that is exactly how \code{PREMISES.md} works.

\smallskip

\subsection*{4. The sweep that was built and never scheduled}
\code{--mode theta} exists, and its discipline is already mechanical: \code{comp\_hash}
\textbf{excludes} theta, so a retune cannot register as a new mechanism --- a change of numbers
cannot masquerade as a new idea because the identity function says so, not because an agent was
asked to be careful. What is missing is only wiring: \code{plan()} emits
\code{recon} and \code{composition} and never \code{theta}; nothing chooses the base composition,
the parameter or the values; and \code{build\_theta\_batch} sweeps \emph{one} parameter of
\emph{one} composition, so mixtures of parameters are not expressible at all. Track~A is not only
``does this mechanism matter'' but ``what does it do as you turn it up'', and the second question
has never been asked.

\begin{resultbox}{8.4 --- A parameter change is a move on the menu \hfill \done}
Cedric's design, and better than the separate mode it replaces: \code{set\_param} becomes an edit
kind beside \code{add\_op}, \code{remove\_op} and \code{set\_impl}, so the Proposer chooses
structural and parameter moves from \emph{one} list and can \textbf{mix them in a single batch} ---
instead of a whole round having to be one or the other.

\smallskip
\textbf{The discipline holds by construction, not by instruction.} \code{comp\_hash} already
excludes parameters, so a retune yields the \emph{same} composition identity and can never register
as a new mechanism. ``A change of numbers can never masquerade as a new idea'' is true because the
identity function says so --- asserted as a test, not asked of an agent.

\smallskip
Two points per parameter, taken from each operator's own \code{(lo, hi, default)}, so a sweep cannot
leave the space the Critic admits. \textbf{The specimen is not an axis}: substrate parameters,
\code{n\_cells} and \code{seed} are excluded, because \code{round.py} grounds starting conditions
for a reason it learned when two slots ran 500 cells against a control at 2000 --- any difference
between them confounded the edit with a fourfold change in specimen size.

\smallskip
\textbf{Structure first, numbers rationed.} Parameter moves outnumbered structural ones seventeen to
seven on the current frontier, so a menu of any length would have shown mostly dials and a batch
would have gone on turning them. Every structural move is offered; parameter moves are capped at a
quarter. Track~A asks whether a mechanism \emph{matters} before it asks what it does as you turn it
up. The preamble now also states what an operator absent from the menu means --- \emph{it does not
exist in this system, however real the biology} --- which is exactly what round~2 needed and was
never told.
\end{resultbox}

\textbf{What you get.} A loop whose parts are declared and checked rather than remembered, with
the sweep arm switched on. \emph{Nothing relaunches until the checker passes} --- and the checker is
now \code{python test\_offline.py}: \textbf{12 cases, 12 passing, no agent and no GPU}.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 9 --- The search could not reach the answer \hfill \prog}

\subsection*{The crux, proved by execution}
\code{apply(("add\_op", ...))} creates the node and \emph{never the connection}.
\code{is\_runnable()} is false for any dangling slot. The Proposer's menu filters on

\noindent Three operators declare slots, and they are the entire morphogen$\to$mechanics arrow:
\code{morphogen\_growth\_3d} (\code{gate}) --- \emph{which is Okuda's own mechanism} ---
\code{extrude} (\code{site}), and \code{divide\_3d:orient\_iface} (\code{axis}). The reviewer ran
the breadth-first closure of the reachable space:

\toprule
reachable runnable compositions from the seed & \textbf{9{,}760} \\
\ldots containing \code{morphogen\_growth\_3d} & 0 \\
\ldots containing \code{extrude} & 0 \\

\subsection*{What that makes of six rounds of findings}
\textbf{The loop's flagship claim is a measurement of its own wiring.} \code{memory.md} records
``chemistry is inert for shape --- SUPPORTED by every \code{set\_impl} react/diffuse/seed and every
chemistry \code{remove\_op}, protr\_peak=1.006''. Twenty runs returned exactly 1.006, including runs
that \emph{deleted the reaction operator outright}. A subsystem with no output edge cannot move a
shape, and the loop filed that as a fact about biology.

\textbf{And the three best results were one artefact seen three times.} The top \code{protr\_peak}
in the campaign, 2.266, appears on three compositions differing only in their reaction block, with
all twenty summary metrics \textbf{bit-identical} and byte-identical VLM captions. Its cause: 95\%
of the vertices collapse to radius 0.08 while a handful fly to 1508, so \code{protr = r95/rmed} is
a ratio of two near-zero numbers. The loop read ``identical across three independent bases'' as
\emph{strength}; it is the signature of a disconnected subsystem. Those three runs also received
three different phenotype labels --- \code{exploded}, \code{spike}, \code{branching} --- from one
bit-identical simulation, and those three words are now the whole phenotype inventory of
\code{lever\_map.md}.

\textbf{The sweep arm was dead on arrival.} \code{comp\_hash} excludes parameters by design, so a
retune cannot register as a new mechanism --- exactly the Track~A discipline, enforced
structurally. But \code{R6\_DUPLICATE} refuses any graph whose hash is already seen, and a
\code{set\_param} child has \emph{the parent's hash}. Measured: \textbf{39 of 39 parameter moves
refused}, even against a seen-list of one. \code{t\_set\_param} passes anyway, because it checks
that the move is \emph{offered} and never that it is \emph{admitted} --- the same interface defect
Phase~8 was built to retire, in Phase~8's own test.

\textbf{The refusal arithmetic, and what it really means.} \code{R6\_DUPLICATE} is 106 of 127
refusals; with \code{DUPLICATE\_IN\_BATCH}, 95\%. But that is the \emph{symptom}: a search that
cannot reach new territory keeps re-proposing the territory it has. Rounds~5 and~6 delivered
\textbf{one run each} --- the same control composition that had already run in rounds 2, 3 and 4 ---
because the control is exempt from the duplicate check and nothing else survived.

\textbf{Track A's discipline is real, and I was wrong to doubt it.} 34 of 46 hypotheses carried a
parseable prediction; all twelve \code{unstated} are round-1 replays where none is owed; of 29
resolved-and-stated, \textbf{29 scored confirmed or refuted and 0 inconclusive}. The register works.
The content is the problem: 19 of 34 predictions were ``nothing will happen'', and 18 of 21
confirmations were \emph{the sphere stayed a sphere}.

\item \textbf{Make \code{add\_op} of a slotted operator atomic with its \code{connect}}, when

\item \textbf{Put the reference recipes on the frontier} rather than treating them as a failure

\item \textbf{The picture must accuse the run.} \code{metrics.png} plotted twelve of fifty-six

\textbf{What you get.} A search space that contains the answer. Everything this project has built

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 10 --- The loop could not say what it was looking at \hfill \prog}
\textbf{Goal.} Cedric, watching a round-2 movie: \emph{``a flash of red activity, 100\% red, then
a long period of white, no activity.''} The loop had no number for that.

It had been running for weeks on a set of measurements that could not tell a real chemical pattern
from a uniform wash of the same average brightness, could not say whether the chemistry was still
alive at the end of a run, and --- as it turned out --- was handing the agent that reads each run
\emph{nothing at all} about how anything changed over time.

\subsection*{Four faults, all the same shape}
Not one is a wrong formula. Each is a thing that was measured and never delivered, or a thing that
was asked for and never measured.

\paragraph{An average cannot see a pattern.} A field that is uniformly grey and a field that is
half bright, half dark have the \emph{same average}. What tells them apart is how much the
brightness \emph{varies from cell to cell}: near zero for the wash, large for the pattern. That
variation was never measured. The loop could only see the brightest cell (\code{act\_max}), which
reads high both for a healthy pattern and for a single cell blowing up, so a pattern that spiked
and a pattern that died looked identical.

On the end state of the run named \code{okuda\_route}, the activator varies by
$8.4\times10^{-5}$ across 3{,}975 cells around an average of $0.0128$. The chemistry is dead.
Every number the loop was allowed to quote stayed perfectly sayable while it died.

\paragraph{The measurement that answers the campaign's own question was never taken.} Everything
here turns on one thing: \emph{does the chemistry drive the shape, or is it decoration on a shape
made by something else?} The number for that --- how strongly a cell's chemical level tracks how
far out it sits (\code{corr\_act\_rad}) --- was on the list of things an agent was allowed to
predict, and was computed \emph{nowhere in the codebase}. A prediction naming it scored ``not
measured'', quietly became ``inconclusive'', and no warning was printed anywhere. The agent wrote
a perfectly good falsifiable claim and the loop recorded that it had learned nothing. Two other
shape measurements were in the same state.

\paragraph{Nothing about time was reaching the reader --- and had not for the whole campaign.}
The per-frame table stores each frame's shape verdict as a \emph{word} (``sphere'', ``tube'').
The code that classifies how each measurement behaves over time tried to convert every column to
numbers, including that one, and crashed on the word. The crash was caught one level up and
turned into a single parenthesis in the reading agent's prompt: \emph{``(trajectory shapes
unavailable)''}.

So since the day that column was added, \textbf{every run has been read with no history at all}:
no warning that a run peaked early and decayed, none that it was still climbing when the clock
ran out, none that it was pinned against a buffer, and no evidence horizon. The curves were
computed, drawn into a figure, and classified --- and the classification died on the one column
that was never meant to be classified.

\paragraph{An instrument that was withdrawn for lying was still being advertised.} One measure of
pattern wavelength was taken off the approved list months ago because it had never been
calibrated. The name stayed on the list, and worse, it was in the short banner that tells every
agent \emph{``these are new, use them''}. It has been renamed to say what it is and marked as
untrustworthy.

\subsection*{What was built}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The pattern, as numbers.} How much the chemical varies across cells
      (\code{act\_sd}); the same divided by the average (\code{act\_cv}), which does not care
      whether the overall level is collapsing or exploding, so it still means something when the
      chemistry is running away; and what fraction of the tissue is switched on
      (\code{act\_occupancy}). A frame counts as \emph{patterned} only if \emph{both} the
      variation and the occupancy are real --- so one cell of four thousand blowing up, which
      makes the variation enormous but the occupancy $0.01$, is correctly not a pattern. Over a
      whole run: what fraction of it had a pattern at all, and the frame at which the pattern
      last died.
\item \textbf{A correlation is refused when there is nothing to correlate.} The chemistry-drives-
      shape number reads a confident $0.294$ on that dead field --- a correlation of rounding
      error, because the statistic is deliberately blind to scale and will happily report on
      noise. It is now simply \emph{not measured} when the pattern is below the liveness
      threshold, which makes ``does the pattern drive the shape'' come out \emph{inconclusive}
      when there is no pattern. That is the honest answer. Given a planted signal --- chemical
      painted onto the outermost 15\% of cells --- the instrument recovers it: $0.724$, and the
      tip-enrichment measure reads $5.19$ against a null of $1.0$.
\item \textbf{Telling a tube from a lumpy ball.} The elongation number in use is a ratio between
      two points of the radius distribution, so it reads the same for one long tube and for a
      broad bumpy sphere. The shape of the whole cell cloud --- long and thin, round, or
      flattened --- now has its own three numbers. This is Okuda's own axis: tube, undulation,
      branch.
\item \textbf{The history channel, revived and widened.} Word-valued columns are skipped instead
      of crashing. The list of curves allowed into a prompt held seven entries and \emph{not one
      about the chemistry}; it now covers shape, cells, pattern and apparatus. And each curve
      carries eight numbers across the run beside its one-word verdict --- because ``peaked''
      describes a gentle rise and fall \emph{and} a spike to seventeen thousand followed by
      extinction, and only the numbers say which. That run now reads
      \code{0 0 0 0 1 0.013 0 0} for the fraction of switched-on cells: the flash Cedric saw,
      written down.
\item \textbf{Three checks so this cannot recur quietly.} Every approved measurement must have
      something that actually computes it; every one must have a written definition, and no
      definition may name a withdrawn measurement; and the history channel must survive a
      word-valued column. Twenty-one checks pass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{One name, one quantity}
A \textbf{plain name} means a per-frame measurement: computed every sampled frame, classified over
time, and reported at the last frame so a prediction can be scored against it. A \textbf{suffix}
is only used when the number is \emph{not} simply the last frame, and says what it is instead ---
the best value reached, or the last \emph{trustworthy} frame before the mesh tore.

Thirteen duplicate names were deleted under that rule. They had been created by copying every
per-frame measurement into the summary twice, so the same number appeared under two names. A set
of measurements is a \emph{vocabulary}: doubling it without adding a quantity makes it harder to
use and measures nothing new. The approved list went from 53 names to 40.

\subsection*{Grouped by the question they answer}
In round 2, all twelve predictions were written about elongation while every chemistry measurement
sat unnamed. The approved list had reached the agent as one comma-separated line of fifty-three
identifiers, and a flat list is a list you read the first entry of.

Every approved measurement now sits in exactly one of five groups, phrased as questions:
\emph{is it a tube; is it still made of cells; is there a pattern at all; does the pattern grip
the shape; is this evidence at all}. Each is printed with the value it takes when the answer is
\emph{no} --- because a prediction against a bare identifier is a guess. ``Variation above 0.3''
means nothing until you know a dead field reads 0.00 and a live one reads about 1. The written
definitions went from 6 to 38.

\subsection*{How often to measure}
The old rule gave \emph{every} run forty samples, however long it was. A 900-frame run was
therefore sampled every 23 frames and a 300-frame run every 8 --- so ``the twelfth sample'' meant a
different moment in each, and two runs could not be compared point by point, which is the one
thing a history is for.

It is now every 25 frames, always, with the last frame added. Measured cost: one frame of analysis
takes $1.43$ seconds on a real 3{,}975-cell mesh, so

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
sampling & frames analysed & cost per run \\
\midrule
old rule (forty samples) & 40 & 57\,s \\
\textbf{every 25 frames} & 37 & \textbf{53\,s} \\
every 10 frames & 91 & 130\,s \\
every frame & 901 & 21.4\,min \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The new rule is \emph{cheaper} than the one it replaces. Measuring every 10 frames would
give two and a half times the resolution in time for about 77 extra seconds a run --- roughly
15 minutes on a twelve-run round --- which is the trade to make if ``when did the pattern die''
needs answering to within ten frames rather than twenty-five.

\subsection*{What is still open}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Which measurements to keep.} The per-frame table produces 64 numbers and much of it
      is one quantity written twice --- three spellings of ``fraction of switched-on cells'', a
      count and a fraction of the same fault, and two different ways of asking whether the
      chemical sits at the tips, one of which was added this week without noticing the other. A
      cut to 24 is drafted and is being checked against every consumer --- a figure panel, a
      biological premise, a shape classifier --- before anything is deleted. One redundancy is
      exact and confirmed; another that looked exact is wrong by 1\% at the precision stored, so
      that measurement stays.
\item \textbf{What to say about time.} With a single per-frame table at a fixed spacing, separate
      measurements for start, middle and end stop being necessary: they become \emph{summaries}
      of one history --- last value, best value, average, spread, trend. Which summaries are
      worth their space in a prompt carrying 24 measurements is the open design question.
\item \textbf{Telling the agents.} Approving a measurement makes a prediction about it
      \emph{scorable}; a separate short list is what tells an agent the measurement \emph{exists}.
      The new chemistry measurements were approved and not announced, so round 2 wrote every
      prediction about elongation while they were computed for the first time and named by
      nobody. The same fault as the others, one list over.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What you get.} A loop that can state what it is looking at. The reviewing agent's
objection to round 2 --- that five of its twelve experiments were reading a shape-to-chemistry
feedback off a field that was already dead --- was, until this phase, an assertion no instrument
could settle. It is now a number.
\end{phasebox}

\subsection*{The number I reported was wrong}

\subsection*{The four faults, each with the number that makes it one}

\paragraph{A sweep was arithmetically impossible.} \code{comp\_hash} is deliberately
parameter-blind so a retune cannot be filed as a new mechanism, and it works: \textbf{107 of 107}
parameter perturbations leave the hash bit-identical. But \code{round.py} deduped slots
\emph{within} a batch on that same bare hash, while \code{proposer.py} \emph{requires} slot~0 to be
the unchanged parent --- so every parameter move collided with the mandatory control.
\textbf{48 of 48} admitted sweep slots were refused \code{DUPLICATE\_IN\_BATCH}, and the refusal
text ``Vary the EDIT, not the number'' then entered the next Proposer's prompt as a lesson. The
loop was teaching itself not to ask Track A's second question. Measured on the live frontier: the
25 sweep edits available share \emph{one} composition hash and have \textbf{25 distinct run keys}.
\code{critic.py} already keyed sweeps on the run key --- mechanism \emph{and} operating point ---
one screen away. Two readings of one identity function disagreed about what a sweep is, and the
stricter one won by being later in the file.

\paragraph{Predictions were scored on specimens the same record called invalid.} The Biologist
writes \code{premises\_broken}; \code{check\_posthoc} never read it. So a run that passed through
itself (P11) or absorbed area by stretching (P7) was scored \code{confirmed} in
\code{hypotheses.jsonl} while \code{collector.py} wrote ``specimen invalid \ldots describes the
configuration and not a tissue'' about the \emph{same} run into \code{analysis.md}. Two records of
one experiment disagreed, and the Supervisor reads the register --- so the surprise rate, the 70/30
steer and the cluster freeze were all computed over runs the Biologist had already rejected.
\textbf{24 of 35} archived runs are refused once the verdict is wired to the resolver.

\paragraph{The loop could not be curious.} \code{INTENTS} held
\code{confirmatory\,|\,adversarial\,|\,control}. \code{Hypothesis(intent="exploratory")} raised
\code{ValueError}, and so did \code{predicted=""}. So every slot had to be a bet, and the trap the
goal names --- \emph{a loop that may only test predictions will only ever propose things it can
predict} --- was structural, not cultural.

\paragraph{Nine of twelve runs were the same mesh under nine names.} Both frontier parents carry
\code{conns: []} and no growth operator, so the chemistry cannot reach the mechanics and the shape
is a \emph{constant function} of the edits being made. Re-measured here: \textbf{10 of 12} round-3
runs share \code{gyr\_prolate\_peak} to sixteen digits across ten distinct compositions. Seven of
them resolved \code{confirmed}.

\subsection*{What the reviewers said is working, and should not be touched}

\subsection*{And the fault none of them was asked about}
Ten of the fifteen runs were refused as \code{P3\_CHEMISTRY\_DIVERGED}, \code{act\_max\_peak}

whole search breeds from, never set it, and \code{sat} was not in the tunable table either, so the
loop could neither set it nor sweep it and every child inherited the divergence. Measured over 250
frames:

\item \textbf{The new metrics caught the second.} \code{act\_max\_peak} $=0.0$,

\item \textbf{The missing piece was an operator, not a number.} \code{cell\_rd\_seed}, present in

\subsection*{What multi-agent genuinely buys, and it is one thing}
Cedric, 5 August: \emph{``the one agent has not much but do not composition.''} That is exactly the
line. The one-agent loop tunes the parameters of a \emph{fixed} model --- the graph, the buffers and
the operators never change, so a proposal cannot be structurally invalid and nothing needs to check
it. This loop \emph{composes}: it chooses which operators exist, how they wire, and how big the
reservoirs are. That creates failure modes the control cannot have, and they are the only ones worth
a gate:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item a spec that does not compile, or wires a slot that the implementation does not expose;
\item a reservoir too small to reach the run's own target --- the arithmetic that voided
      \textbf{59 runs} in two batches, every one ending at exactly 1778 cells;
\item a composition already evaluated, spending a GPU on a duplicate.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Those are \emph{structural}, cheap, and decidable before a GPU is touched. Everything else
this loop refuses, it refuses on \emph{judgement about a result} --- and that is where it went wrong.

\subsection*{Premises: an input, not a gate}
Cedric, 5 August: \emph{``I like the premise.md but as an input not a gate.''} The premise battery
produces the best diagnostic text in the system --- \emph{``volume went 522.1 $\to$ 312.9''},
\emph{``the top 5\% of cells reach shape index 5.83''}, \emph{``the surface folds through itself at
frame 50''} --- and every word of it was spent on a \textbf{refusal}. I wired it into the resolver on
4 August and it stopped the campaign: P1, P7 and P11 fail on essentially every composition-mode run,
so 12 of 12 were refused in two consecutive rounds, both aborted, and the loop halted itself. Its own
message was correct: \emph{``the refusal reasons are not actionable.''}

There is a clean division of labour the gate was blurring, and keeping it makes the removal safe:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{the evidence horizon} decides which \emph{frames} are trustworthy. Arithmetic, per
      frame, already truncating everything measured after the mesh tears. \emph{This is the
      mechanism that stops a number being believed, and it is untouched.}
\item \textbf{the premises} say what is biologically wrong, in words carrying numbers. Their
      consumer is \emph{the next proposal}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The audit's actual finding was narrower than I read it: \code{hypotheses.jsonl} said
\code{confirmed} while \code{analysis.md} said \emph{specimen invalid} about the same run --- two
records \emph{disagreeing}. The fix is to write the verdict beside the outcome
(\code{confirmed [specimen: P1, P7, P11 broken]}) so a reader can weigh it. Refusing the run instead
threw away the diagnosis along with the evidence.

where refining a role means editing code and re-reading a docstring to find out what the role was
told.

So: one script and one markdown file per role, and nothing else in \code{agents/}.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llp{5.6cm}}
\toprule
\code{proposer.py} & \code{proposer.md} & what to try next, and what makes a good experiment \\
\code{analyst.py}  & \code{analyst.md}  & how to read a batch: metrics, the eye's captions, the

\code{eye.py}      & \code{eye.md}      & what to look for in a movie, and to say plainly when the

\code{grounder.py} & \code{grounder.md} & Okuda's figures and settings, and the comparison of
                                          \emph{this} round's result against them \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent \textbf{The split is the point.} The \code{.md} is the \emph{role} --- stable, in the
user's hands, versioned in git, edited between rounds without a Python change. The \code{.py} is only
\emph{plumbing}: gather the data, inject it, call, parse, validate. A prompt built as
\code{md + injected data} also makes the prompt readable as a file, which no amount of docstring
achieves.

\textbf{And this is what makes the Grounder the fourth agent rather than dead code.} It was never
badly designed --- 766 lines, 16 functions, 4 ever called, and the two that would compare a result to
the paper (\code{gate()}, \code{figure\_target()}) called \textbf{zero} times. Its repeated $\phi$
note is a decision demanded of nobody, printed forever. Moved to Act 3 with its knowledge in
\code{grounder.md}, it answers the question Cedric asked weeks ago and the loop has never once
asked: \emph{does this look like Okuda's figure?} When it is wrong or repetitive, the fix is to edit
279 lines of markdown, not to re-wire 766 lines of Python.

\subsection*{The engine is blind to the roles}
Cedric, 5 August: \emph{``can we have the round engine kind of blind to the role of the agents?''}

Yes --- and this is the structural cause of the 657-line function, not merely a tidier design.
\code{\_run\_round} grew because it \emph{knows every role by name}: a retry loop written for the
Proposer, a budget carve-out for the Reader, a repair pass for one failure mode, an escalation path
for another. Every role that was added arrived with a special case in the engine. \textbf{An engine
that cannot name a role cannot grow one.}

So the engine knows three \emph{stages} and no roles at all:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llp{5.9cm}}
\toprule
stage & fan-out & the stage's contract \\
\midrule
\code{propose} & one call  & returns a list of edits \\
\code{observe} & per run   & returns text about one run \\
\code{review}  & one call  & returns text about the batch \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Each role is a directory entry, not a branch in the engine. Its \code{.py} declares where
it attaches and what it needs; its \code{.md} is the prose:

\begin{verbatim}
ROLE = {"stage": "review", "wants": ["metrics", "observations", "history"],
        "writes": "grounding.md", "md": "grounder.md"}

def run(bundle):  ...
\end{verbatim}

\noindent and the engine is the whole of this:

\begin{verbatim}
for a in roles_at("propose"): edits = a.run(bundle)
specs = [build(e) for e in edits]          # emit, critic.admit, write yaml
runs  = launch(specs)                      # the engine's own work
data  = [measure(r) for r in runs]         # metrics module, unchanged
for a in roles_at("observe"):
    for r in runs: a.run(bundle | {"run": r})
for a in roles_at("review"): a.run(bundle | {"metrics": data})
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{What this buys, concretely.} Adding or removing an agent is adding or removing two files.
Dropping the eye, or adding a fifth role next month, requires \emph{no edit to the engine} --- which
is the test this design has to pass, because the eleven roles removed in this phase were each added
by editing the engine. The words \code{proposer}, \code{analyst}, \code{eye} and \code{grounder}
should not appear in \code{round.py} at all, and that is checkable in one grep.

\textbf{The one piece of role knowledge that cannot be removed, stated honestly.} Something must
return \emph{edits}, because the engine has to build a spec from them. That knowledge belongs to the
\emph{stage contract} --- \code{propose} means ``returns edits'' --- and not to any role, so any file
may occupy that stage and a round with nothing in it is simply a no-op rather than a crash. The same
holds for \code{observe} and \code{review}: they return text, the engine files it, and the engine
never inspects what it says. A role that produces something no stage consumes is then impossible to
write, which is the defect this campaign hit six times.

\subsection*{And an instruction file for the round}
Cedric, 5 August: \emph{``and an instruction file for round?''}

Yes, and it half exists already --- conflated. \code{campaign/instruction.md} is 63 lines, tracked in
git, and its third section reads \emph{``\#\# What you are --- You are the PROPOSER.''} The
\emph{campaign's} objective and \emph{one role's} identity live in the same file. That is why the
Proposer is the only role that has ever had standing instructions a human could edit: the campaign
file \emph{was} its prompt, and the other fourteen roles had nothing but f-strings.

Split into two layers, five files:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{7.3cm}}
\toprule
\code{round.md} & \textbf{shown to every role.} The objective; the discipline (a change of numbers is
not a hypothesis; every candidate carries a falsifiable prediction; $\sim$70/30 confirmatory to
adversarial); what is already known; what has been ruled out and must not be re-proposed. \\
\midrule
\code{proposer.md}, \code{analyst.md}, \code{eye.md}, \code{grounder.md} &
\textbf{shown to one role.} How that role does its job, and nothing about the campaign. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Every prompt is then \code{round.md} $+$ \code{<role>.md} $+$ injected data, assembled by
the engine in that order. The engine \emph{reads} both files and interprets neither, which keeps it
blind: it cannot behave differently for one role because it never learns which role it is holding.

\textbf{One line I will not cross: the round's numbers stay in config, not in markdown.} Slots per
round, the control slot, the launch budget, the analysis stride and the stop rule are values the
engine must \emph{obey}; a number the engine has to parse out of prose is a number that can silently
drift from the number that ran. \code{round.md} carries the \emph{procedure and the judgement}, and
\code{config} carries the \emph{quantities} --- the same division that makes the metric bank
trustworthy.

\textbf{Where the knowledge accumulates, which is the whole reason the control works.} The Analyst
writes \code{knowledge.md} each round; you fold what survives into \code{round.md} between rounds.
That is precisely the cycle running in \code{connectome-gnn-cx}, where 279 lines of hand-edited
markdown are carrying months of judgement, and it is the one mechanism this loop has never had:
fifteen roles, and not one file a human could edit to make the next round smarter.

\subsection*{The round is a graph, and the engine only runs it}
Cedric, 5 August: \emph{``what about a graph provided to the round with agents and information flow so
that it is blind ... that would be easy to modify, easy to understand instead of hardcoding?''}

Better than the three hard-coded stages I had built an hour earlier, for a reason specific to this
campaign. In the stage version each role declared a \code{wants} list --- and the engine passed the
whole context regardless, so the declaration was decoration. Nothing could check it. As a graph the
information flow \emph{is} the artefact, so \textbf{the defect this loop hit at least six times
becomes arithmetic}: a node that emits something no \code{in:} names is refused at load, before a GPU
or a token is spent.

\code{crew/flow.yaml} is 120 lines and is the whole multi-agent design --- not a diagram of it:

\begin{verbatim}
  - id: diagnosis            # why last round's tissue broke, ranked
    code: engine.diagnosis
    in: [parents]

  - id: proposer
    agent: proposer
    in: [parents, menu, metric_bank, diagnosis, history]
    out: edits

  - id: eye                  # one call per run, on the movie
    agent: eye
    in: [metrics]
    each: names
    out: observed

  - id: analyst
    agent: analyst
    in: [control, metrics, predictions, observations, observed, history]
\end{verbatim}

\noindent Seventeen nodes: thirteen \code{code:} steps and the four \code{agent:} roles. The engine
loads it, checks it, sorts it topologically and executes it, and holds no opinion about what any node
is for. \code{engine.py} is \textbf{481 lines} and \code{test\_engine.py::blind} asserts that no
role's name appears in its executable code, so the assertion fails the moment a special case is
written for one of them.

\textbf{Four checks at load, and the third is why the graph earns its indirection.}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{7.1cm}}
\toprule
every \code{in:} is emitted & otherwise a role runs on missing data and says something confident
about nothing \\
no cycles & arithmetic \\
\textbf{every emission is consumed} & unless the node is terminal. \code{steer} never reached the
Proposer; the premise diagnosis was spent on a refusal; \code{sat} was set in two places and emitted
in none; the eye disagreed with the Reader on 2 of 10 runs and nothing compared them. All found by
hand, weeks later. \\
targets resolve & a typo in the flow is not a silent no-op \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent \textbf{The check fired on the flow's first load and on me twice.} First: \emph{```record'
emits `record' and NO node consumes it''} --- correct, and the honest answer was that its output is a
file, so a terminal node now declares \code{writes:} rather than being exempted by the engine
guessing. Second, while writing the test that drops the eye: deleting its node left \code{observed} in
two roles' \code{in:} lists, and the loader refused with \emph{```analyst' needs `observed', which no
node emits.''} That is exactly the half-finished edit a human makes, so the test now asserts both
halves --- the incomplete removal is refused, the complete one loads.

\begin{verbatim}
@register
class ActCV(Metric):
    name   = "act_cv"
    group  = "chemistry"                  # which question it answers
    note   = "activity CV -- scale-free, survives a collapsing or exploding level"
    series = True                         # -> the six temporal reductions
    refuse_if = "act_cv <= 0.05"          # the guard that is inline today
    def compute(self, f):
        act = f.activity
        return act.std() / abs(act.mean()) if abs(act.mean()) > 1e-12 else 0.0
\end{verbatim}

\noindent One class replaces six registrations, and the registry \emph{derives} what is
hand-maintained now: \code{registry.names()} for the 156 admitted names,
\code{registry.notes()} for the bank the Proposer is handed, the \code{group} field for
\code{METRIC\_GROUP}, \code{@withdrawn("reason")} for the 114, \code{registry.compute\_frame(state)}
for the \code{frame\_metrics} monolith, and \code{refuse\_if} for the guards written inline --- the
one that says \code{corr\_act\_rad} must not be computed on a dead field.

\textbf{On pointing the metrics from \code{analyst.md}, with one change.} The md should name the
\emph{questions}, not the list. If it carried the 156 names they would drift from the registry, and a
metric list that has stopped describing the code is this campaign's most reliable defect --- a limit
in a comment, a paper's own $\phi$, a rail read as a result. So \code{analyst.md} says \emph{``lead
with protrusion: \code{protr\_peak}, \code{n\_tubes}, \code{protrusion\_aspect\_max}''} and the
registry stays the source of truth, with a test asserting that \textbf{every metric named in any
\code{.md} exists in the registry}. Prose may point; it may never define. Same rule as the flow
check, and the same reason.

\textbf{Not while a round is live.} \code{cluster.py} maps \code{/workspace} to

jobs now at frame 110 of 900. Editing it mid-round would reproduce the defect that broke the Table~1

\section*{Appendix --- words I could not avoid}

Only these four. Everything else above is ordinary English.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cell sheet (or mesh)} --- the model of the tissue: a hollow ball of polygonal cells
sharing walls, like a football.
\item \textbf{Operator} --- one mechanism as a reusable building block: ``cells divide'',
``chemical spreads'', ``surface pulls tight''. A model is a combination of these.
\item \textbf{The loop} --- the automated cycle: propose a change, predict the outcome, run,
measure, record whether the prediction held.
\item \textbf{Phase} --- one of the eight stages above. Our agreed stopping points.
\end{itemize}
