\section{Why a second repository, and why this one}

One repository cannot show saturation. The measurement \texttt{plexus2.tex} App.~E.1 asks for ---
\emph{does decomposing a broad collection of frameworks converge on a compact reusable
vocabulary?} --- is a claim about what happens \emph{across} frameworks. With $n=1$ the curve is a
shape, not an argument.

\textbf{Target: CompuCell3D (Cellular Potts).} Nominated by the jax-morph note before any of this
work, on the grounds that it is \emph{the biggest step away from everything we have}: a cell is not
a point with per-cell state, it is a \textbf{set of lattice sites}. Its position is a derived
centroid. Its dynamics are Metropolis flips of site ownership. There is no per-cell equation to
subtract.

That is the point. Every measurement in the jax-morph atlas rested on cells being points and a
differential test being a subtraction. \textbf{If the procedure is general it survives this; if it
is secretly a particle-framework procedure, this is where it breaks.}

The prediction --- made before running anything, and the reason this note exists --- is in
\code{TRANSFER.md}.

\textbf{Getting there meant routing around three defects in CompuCell3D 4.10.0}, none of them
configuration:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item The advertised pure-Python route (\code{PyCoreSpecs} $\to$ \code{service\_cc3d}) passes the
\emph{list of specs} where a simulation file is expected:
\code{get\_custom\_settings\_path()} does \code{Path(<list>)}. Shimming that only exposes the next
one --- \code{CC3DSimService.\_run} asserts \code{os.path.isfile(<list>)}. \textbf{That route
simply does not work in this release.}
\item CC3D \code{exec}s the project's Python script with its own globals, so a steppable class
defined in the same file raises \code{NameError: SteppableBasePy is not defined} at registration.
The class must live in a separate importable module.
\item The output directory may not sit inside the project folder.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The way through} is to use \code{PyCoreSpecs} for what it is genuinely good at ---
generating correct CC3DML, \code{<RandomSeed>} included --- write a real \code{.cc3d} project, and
run it through \code{cc3d/run\_script.py}, the officially supported headless entry point, which
never touches the broken path. The model stays defined in Python; only the transport is a file.

\textbf{Why this mattered enough to spend the time on.} jax-morph was chosen \emph{because} it ran,
and the first note said so in its own Phase 0. A target that cannot be run reduces the atlas to the
Okuda situation --- wrong operator, wrong parameter or wrong reading of a figure, with no way to
tell which. \textbf{Determinism is the gate behind the gate}: without it, every later disagreement
is unattributable between their noise and our error. Both now hold, so Phase 1 is unblocked.

\section{The phases}

Seven stages, the same ladder as the first atlas. \textbf{I stop at the end of each one and wait
for you.} What differs is not the procedure but what each stage has to mean when a cell is a
region of a lattice rather than a point.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 0 --- Instruments and the oracle \hfill \done}
\textbf{Goal.} Make every later measurement trustworthy before taking one.

The instruments came across for free (\S2). \textbf{The oracle was the phase}, and it is where the
risk always was: install, import, isolate, run headless, and be deterministic at a fixed seed.

\textbf{All six gates now pass} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}), including the stop condition --- the authors'
own cell-sorting model runs twice from one seed and is bit-identical. Until that held, a later
disagreement would have been unattributable between their noise and our error. It holds.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 1 --- Read every mechanism \hfill \done}
The excavator on each mechanism: what it does to the state, its equations, the role of every knob,
and the \code{surprises} a reimplementer gets wrong.

\textbf{What is different here.} jax-morph's mechanisms were 20 classes plus 4 architectural
contracts a scan could not see. CompuCell3D's are \emph{plugins} and \emph{steppables} declared
through XML or \code{PyCoreSpecs} --- a different extraction problem, and one where the
scan-versus-hand split will fall in a different place. \textbf{The 4 hand-added entries were among
the most interesting things the first atlas found}; expecting an automatic scan to be sufficient
here would be repeating a mistake we already know about.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 1 --- what actually happened \hfill \done\ (35 of 35)}
\textbf{The inventory is complete and it is bigger than the first atlas's:} \textbf{35
mechanisms}, against jax-morph's 24. Thirty come from \code{cc3d.core.PyCoreSpecs} --- the
framework's \emph{own} registry of what a model can switch on, which is a better source than a
syntax-tree scan because it is the project's index rather than our reading of it.

\textbf{Five were added by hand, and they are the interesting ones.} The first atlas found that 4
of its 24 were not classes; here the same is true and more so, because a Cellular Potts model's
defining commitments are not plugins:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{9.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\code{cell\_as\_lattice\_domain} & a cell is a set of lattice sites sharing an id; volume is a
count, position a derived centroid\\
\code{metropolis\_acceptance} & $P = 1$ if $\Delta E \le 0$ else $e^{-\Delta E/T}$ --- discrete,
stochastic, and with no pathwise derivative\\
\code{energy\_sum\_composition} & mechanisms compose by \emph{adding energy terms}, not by
operator splitting. The direct counterpart of jax-morph's Lie--Trotter split, and the contrast is
itself a finding\\
\code{mcs\_time\_unit} & one MCS is one attempted copy per site. \textbf{There is no \code{dt}}\\
\code{pixel\_neighbourhood} & the model's relation is a lattice adjacency, not an edge set built
from positions\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{All 35 are now at \emph{inspected}}: 0 candidates, 0 validator violations, 0 blocked. The
excavator read each mechanism at source in the installed package --- 25 agent calls at roughly 4
minutes each, none reverted for a bad verdict.

\textbf{The prompts had to be retargeted first, and that is the part worth recording.} The forked
\code{agents/} still described jax-morph: its paper, its extracted text, its guides directory.
Running it unchanged would have sent 24 agents looking for \code{papers/jax-morph}. The preamble
now describes a Cellular Potts framework, says where the source actually is (an absolute
\code{file:Lnn} into the installed package), states plainly that \textbf{we have no extracted
paper text} --- ``do not cite page numbers you have not read'' --- and adds a rule the first
campaign never needed: \emph{most CC3D mechanisms are energy terms, not updates; say so in
\code{state\_io} rather than forcing them into read/write language that does not fit.}

\textbf{The record is checkable.} \code{R3\_code\_path} resolves for every entry because each
carries a real \code{file:line}. The first seeding used dotted module names and R3 rejected all 11
--- correctly, and that is the rule doing its job rather than a nuisance.

\textbf{Eight mechanisms were BLOCKED and then unblocked, with the reason logged.} Three agents
changed nothing (a call that produces no record change produced nothing), one wrote YAML that
stopped parsing, one timed out, and two touched \code{atlas\_record.yaml} outside the driver and
were restored. Every one was completed on a later call. A stale block is its own kind of lie ---
\code{status} keeps reporting work outstanding that is done --- so each clearance is written to
\code{\_state/unblocked.jsonl} with the evidence that justified it (equations, \code{state\_io},
and a note file all present), rather than quietly deleted.

\textbf{The excavations are real work, not summaries.} \code{diffusionsolverfe}'s update is
transcribed from the OpenCL kernel at L954--L1054; \code{curvature} is identified as the Menger
curvature $\kappa = 2\sin\theta/|R-L|$ of three junction-linked centres of mass;
\code{contactlocalflex} is traced through the compiled core to a per-cell
\code{ContactLocalFlexDataContainer} that the identical Python constructor does not reveal.

\textbf{What the six runs already establish}, each with its control (see \S\ref{sec:demos}): every
CPM mechanism here is an \emph{energy term}, not an update. None of them writes state; they change
only the probability that a proposed pixel copy is accepted. That is a structural fact about the
whole framework, and it is what Phase 2 will have to normalize against a language whose operators
return deltas.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 2 --- Normalize, and let the skeptic attack \hfill \done}
Each mechanism becomes a typed Plexus contract with a verdict, then the skeptic tries to break it.

\textbf{The prediction is already on the record} (\code{TRANSFER.md}): the \emph{biological}
mechanisms --- growth, division, death, chemotaxis, secretion, diffusion --- should come back
largely \code{alias} or \code{implementation} of contracts the first atlas already registered,
while the \emph{representational} ones --- cell-as-domain, the Metropolis acceptance rule, contact
energy over shared boundary, volume and surface constraints --- should be genuinely new. If that
splits as predicted, the conclusion is that \emph{the biological vocabulary is converging while
the representational vocabulary is not}.
\end{phasebox}

\textbf{The second repository yields FEWER new contracts per mechanism than the first} --- 0.33
against 0.44 --- which is the direction the saturation hypothesis predicts and was not guaranteed.
It is one data point, not a curve.

\textbf{The cross-repository fix earned itself immediately.} Without \code{-{}-prior}, CompuCell3D
scores \textbf{9} new contracts and re-counts \code{adhere} as a first sighting. With it, the four
contact plugins (\code{Contact}, \code{ContactLocalFlex}, \code{ContactInternal},
\code{AdhesionFlex}) are correctly classified as \emph{implementations} of the \code{adhere}
contract jax-morph already proposed. \textbf{One contract, double-counted, is a 12\% error on the
headline} --- and it would have grown with every further repository.

\textbf{The normalizer found the strongest case itself.} On the last mechanism it reported that
CompuCell3D's \code{SteadyStateDiffusionSolver} solves \emph{the identical equation} as
jax-morph's \code{morphogen} --- $D\nabla^2 c - kc + S = 0$ --- and classified it as a second
sighting rather than new vocabulary. That is the convergence claim showing up as a concrete
match between two unrelated codebases, not as an assertion.

\textbf{What is genuinely new here is representational, as predicted.} \code{occupy} --- a cell as
a set of lattice sites --- is new, and so are \code{volume\_elasticity},
\code{membrane\_tension}, \code{elongate}, \code{stiffen}, \code{bond}, \code{stay\_connected} and
\code{react}. \textbf{The prediction in \code{TRANSFER.md} was half right}: the biological
mechanisms did collapse into contracts the first atlas already had, and the representation did
yield something new. But three of the five architectural entries --- the Metropolis rule, the
energy-sum composition and the MCS time unit --- came back \emph{out of scope} rather than new:
the normalizer judged them framework mechanics with no biology, exactly as jax-morph's four
architectural entries were judged. \textbf{Two frameworks, two very different architectures, and
both times the architecture scored zero new biological vocabulary.}

\textbf{The skeptic earned its place again.} Every \code{new} claim was challenged --- 14
mechanisms, since the record held more \code{new} verdicts than the ledger counts distinct
contracts. \textbf{Five were refuted and sent back to the normalizer}: \code{adhesionflex},
\code{connectivity}, \code{lengthconstraint}, \code{surface} and \code{volume}. \code{connectivity}
was demoted outright from \code{new} to \code{alias}. Nine survived, at confidences of 0.65--0.85.

\textbf{And a driver lesson worth recording.} The first attempt ran
\code{step -{}-role normalizer -{}-skeptic} over the already-normalized entries, which re-runs the
NORMALIZER first --- and a call that changes nothing counts as a failure. Nothing was corrupted
(the guard rejected the no-op edits and the measurement was unchanged), but only one challenge
actually landed. \code{-{}-skeptic} belongs \emph{during} the normalizing pass; afterwards, the
skeptic must be invoked as its own role.

\textbf{Eleven out-of-scope is nearly half the framework}, against six of twenty-four for
jax-morph. A Potts framework carries far more machinery that models no biology --- trackers,
initialisers, dumpers, the acceptance rule itself.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 3--7 --- DEFERRED, deliberately \hfill \todo}
\textbf{The order changed, and this is the reason.} The original plan ran all seven phases per
repository: read, normalize, implement, validate, figure, differentiate, promote. That is the
right order for \emph{one} target and the wrong order for the programme.

\textbf{Extraction is cheap and reversible; committing the language is not.} Phases 1--2 only read
and classify --- they write a record and nothing else. Phase 3 writes operator modules into
\code{src/plexus/operators/candidates/}, and Phase 7 promotes them into the language proper.
Implementing CompuCell3D's sixteen candidates now would shape the anti-chamber around \emph{one}
framework before we know what the next three need --- and the catalog is precisely the instrument
that tells us that.

\textbf{So the revised programme is:}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Extract} (Phases 0--2) across many repositories. Each yields a record and a
contribution to the ledger. Cheap, parallel, and nothing is committed.
\item \textbf{Catalog} (\code{catalog.py}) --- merge every record, count each contract ONCE across
repositories, and read the cumulative-new curve. This is the only place the App.~E.1 claim can
actually be tested.
\item \textbf{Then} implement, validate and promote --- on the contracts the catalog says survive
contact with more than one framework.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What defers with them.} Phase 4's differential tests need implementations, so they wait
too. Phase 6 (differentiability) was in any case likely inapplicable here --- a Metropolis
accept/reject has no pathwise derivative --- and saying so is a finding rather than a gap.

\textbf{What this costs.} The record's \code{status} ladder will sit at \emph{normalized} for every
repository, and the ledger's verdicts stay unvalidated against the reference. That is a real
limitation and it is the price of not guessing: \textbf{a verdict of `new' that no differential
test has checked is a claim about our reading of the source, not about the source.}
\end{phasebox}

\textbf{Every ablation separates, and several reverse rather than merely weaken}: sorting becomes

applying to an entire framework.

volume parameters silently disabling per-cell growth; type-colouring hiding every cell; a
chemotaxis strength that transported cells into the wall and destroyed them; and the
\code{PyCoreSpecs} API not being spelled like CC3DML.

\textbf{A contract that only ever appears in the repository that introduced it is a description of
that repository. One that reappears independently is evidence the vocabulary is real.} The
\code{morphogen} case is the sharpest: two unrelated codebases, one written in JAX around point
particles and one a C++ lattice framework, solving the same equation
$D\nabla^2 c - kc + S = 0$ --- and the normalizer identified the match itself.

\textbf{What would refute the programme} is the curve failing to flatten as repositories are
added: if each new framework keeps contributing 8 contracts nobody else needs, the algebra is
cataloguing our naming habits rather than converging on biology. That is measurable, and it is
what the next few extractions are for.

\section{An instrument defect only the second repository could reveal --- fixed}\label{sec:gap}

\code{saturation.py} classifies a mechanism \code{implementation} when it reaches a contract
\emph{this run} has already counted as new. At $n=1$ that is complete. At $n=2$ it is wrong: every
contract the first atlas introduced would be counted as new \emph{again} by the second, inflating
precisely the number the instrument exists to protect.

\textbf{The fix is a \code{-{}-prior} flag}: an earlier \code{atlas\_record.yaml} pre-seeds the
"already spoken for" set, so a second sighting reads as an implementation \emph{of that
repository} rather than as new vocabulary. Checked by feeding jax-morph's record back to itself ---
its 8 new contracts all demote and it scores \textbf{NEW 0, implementation 14}, which is the
correct answer for a repository measured against itself.

Applied to \emph{both} campaigns' copies, so the instrument stays one instrument. That is the whole
value of a second target arriving before the first is promoted: \textbf{the defect was invisible
at $n=1$ and structural at $n=2$}, and it would have silently doubled the headline number.
