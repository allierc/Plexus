\section{Why a second repository, and why this one}

One repository cannot show saturation. The measurement \texttt{plexus2.tex} App.~E.1 asks for ---
\emph{does decomposing a broad collection of frameworks converge on a compact reusable
vocabulary?} --- is a claim about what happens \emph{across} frameworks. With $n=1$ the curve is a
shape, not an argument.

\textbf{Target: CompuCell3D (Cellular Potts).} Nominated by the jax-morph note before any of this
work, on the grounds that it is \emph{the biggest step away from everything we have}: a cell is not
a point with per-cell state, it is a \textbf{set of lattice sites}. Its position is a derived
centroid. Its dynamics are Metropolis flips of site ownership. There is no per-cell equation to
subtract.

That is the point. Every measurement in the jax-morph atlas rested on cells being points and a
differential test being a subtraction. \textbf{If the procedure is general it survives this; if it
is secretly a particle-framework procedure, this is where it breaks.}

The prediction --- made before running anything, and the reason this note exists --- is in
\code{TRANSFER.md}.

\section{The oracle --- where the risk actually is}\label{sec:oracle}

\code{TRANSFER.md} predicted the oracle would be the largest risk, and a \emph{target} risk rather
than a \emph{procedure} risk. It was both, and \textbf{it now passes}.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l p{8.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{gate} & \textbf{state} & \\
\midrule
installs & \dn & CompuCell3D 4.10.0 from its own conda channel into an isolated environment
(\code{/workspace/.conda\_envs/cc3d-oracle}), py312.\\
imports & \dn & after supplying \code{libGL} --- the container is headless and CompuCell3D links
its graphics stack unconditionally.\\
model definable in Python & \dn & \code{cc3d.core.PyCoreSpecs}, so the model is written in Python
and never hand-typed as XML.\\
\textbf{isolation} & \dn & the Plexus interpreter \emph{cannot} \code{import cc3d}. Checked, not
assumed: a process that can reach the reference can borrow its answer.\\
\textbf{runs headless} & \dn & 9 cells at $30^2$, 45 at $60^2$; artefacts with provenance in
\code{\_oracle/runs/smoke/}.\\
\textbf{deterministic at a fixed seed} & \dn & two runs at seed 42 are \emph{bit-identical} across
id, type, volume, surface and centre of mass; seed 7 differs.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Getting there meant routing around three defects in CompuCell3D 4.10.0}, none of them
configuration:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item The advertised pure-Python route (\code{PyCoreSpecs} $\to$ \code{service\_cc3d}) passes the
\emph{list of specs} where a simulation file is expected:
\code{get\_custom\_settings\_path()} does \code{Path(<list>)}. Shimming that only exposes the next
one --- \code{CC3DSimService.\_run} asserts \code{os.path.isfile(<list>)}. \textbf{That route
simply does not work in this release.}
\item CC3D \code{exec}s the project's Python script with its own globals, so a steppable class
defined in the same file raises \code{NameError: SteppableBasePy is not defined} at registration.
The class must live in a separate importable module.
\item The output directory may not sit inside the project folder.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The way through} is to use \code{PyCoreSpecs} for what it is genuinely good at ---
generating correct CC3DML, \code{<RandomSeed>} included --- write a real \code{.cc3d} project, and
run it through \code{cc3d/run\_script.py}, the officially supported headless entry point, which
never touches the broken path. The model stays defined in Python; only the transport is a file.

\textbf{Why this mattered enough to spend the time on.} jax-morph was chosen \emph{because} it ran,
and the first note said so in its own Phase 0. A target that cannot be run reduces the atlas to the
Okuda situation --- wrong operator, wrong parameter or wrong reading of a figure, with no way to
tell which. \textbf{Determinism is the gate behind the gate}: without it, every later disagreement
is unattributable between their noise and our error. Both now hold, so Phase 1 is unblocked.

\section{The phases}

Seven stages, the same ladder as the first atlas. \textbf{I stop at the end of each one and wait
for you.} What differs is not the procedure but what each stage has to mean when a cell is a
region of a lattice rather than a point.

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 0 --- Instruments and the oracle \hfill \done}
\textbf{Goal.} Make every later measurement trustworthy before taking one.

The instruments came across for free (\S2). \textbf{The oracle was the phase}, and it is where the
risk always was: install, import, isolate, run headless, and be deterministic at a fixed seed.

\textbf{All six gates now pass} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}), including the stop condition --- the authors'
own cell-sorting model runs twice from one seed and is bit-identical. Until that held, a later
disagreement would have been unattributable between their noise and our error. It holds.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 1 --- Read every mechanism \hfill \ns}
The excavator on each mechanism: what it does to the state, its equations, the role of every knob,
and the \code{surprises} a reimplementer gets wrong.

\textbf{What is different here.} jax-morph's mechanisms were 20 classes plus 4 architectural
contracts a scan could not see. CompuCell3D's are \emph{plugins} and \emph{steppables} declared
through XML or \code{PyCoreSpecs} --- a different extraction problem, and one where the
scan-versus-hand split will fall in a different place. \textbf{The 4 hand-added entries were among
the most interesting things the first atlas found}; expecting an automatic scan to be sufficient
here would be repeating a mistake we already know about.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 2 --- Normalize, and let the skeptic attack \hfill \ns}
Each mechanism becomes a typed Plexus contract with a verdict, then the skeptic tries to break it.

\textbf{The prediction is already on the record} (\code{TRANSFER.md}): the \emph{biological}
mechanisms --- growth, division, death, chemotaxis, secretion, diffusion --- should come back
largely \code{alias} or \code{implementation} of contracts the first atlas already registered,
while the \emph{representational} ones --- cell-as-domain, the Metropolis acceptance rule, contact
energy over shared boundary, volume and surface constraints --- should be genuinely new. If that
splits as predicted, the conclusion is that \emph{the biological vocabulary is converging while
the representational vocabulary is not}.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 3 --- Implement what is missing \hfill \ns}
Every \code{new} and every \code{refinement} becomes a Plexus operator in the anti-chamber, with a
test of one property statable without the reference: a conservation law, a sign, a limit, a
symmetry.

\textbf{The open question this phase will force.} Plexus's \code{cell} set is points with per-cell
state. A Potts cell is a \emph{set of lattice sites}. Either that is expressible as an existing
set with a different map, or the language needs a representation it does not have --- and finding
out which is worth more than any individual operator here.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 4 --- Differential validation \hfill \ns}
Metric and threshold written down \emph{first}; then the same initial condition through both
implementations; then subtract.

\textbf{This is the phase most likely to break, and the reason this target was chosen.} Every
differential test in the first atlas was a subtraction of per-cell arrays. Here there is no
per-cell equation to compare and the dynamics are a Metropolis chain, so a matched trajectory is
not even meaningful --- the same argument that made the division differ compare a pooled
\emph{hazard} instead of cell counts, but now applying to the whole framework rather than one
operator. The tests will have to be \textbf{distributional}: equilibrium sorted-boundary length,
volume distribution, contact-energy spectrum.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 5 --- The forward figure \hfill \ns}
One headline result of the paper reproduced inside Plexus out of normalized operators, and set
\emph{beside} the reference rather than described next to it. Cell sorting is the obvious
candidate: it is CompuCell3D's canonical demonstration and its outcome is a topology, not a
trajectory.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 6 --- Differentiability \hfill \ns}
\textbf{Probably not applicable, and saying why is the result.} The first atlas's Phase 6 rested on
the forward model being differentiable. A Metropolis acceptance is a discrete accept/reject on an
energy difference --- there is no pathwise gradient through it at all, and the straight-through
trick that carried the division draw does not obviously transfer. If Plexus can express Potts
dynamics forward but not inversely, that is a real and reportable limit of the language.
\end{phasebox}

\begin{phasebox}{Phase 7 --- Promotion, and the joint ledger \hfill \ns}
The measurement only becomes an argument here: the cumulative-new curve across \emph{two}
repositories. Flat means the language is saturating; still climbing means it is not.
\end{phasebox}

\section{An instrument defect only the second repository could reveal --- fixed}\label{sec:gap}

\code{saturation.py} classifies a mechanism \code{implementation} when it reaches a contract
\emph{this run} has already counted as new. At $n=1$ that is complete. At $n=2$ it is wrong: every
contract the first atlas introduced would be counted as new \emph{again} by the second, inflating
precisely the number the instrument exists to protect.

\textbf{The fix is a \code{-{}-prior} flag}: an earlier \code{atlas\_record.yaml} pre-seeds the
"already spoken for" set, so a second sighting reads as an implementation \emph{of that
repository} rather than as new vocabulary. Checked by feeding jax-morph's record back to itself ---
its 8 new contracts all demote and it scores \textbf{NEW 0, implementation 14}, which is the
correct answer for a repository measured against itself.

Applied to \emph{both} campaigns' copies, so the instrument stays one instrument. That is the whole
value of a second target arriving before the first is promoted: \textbf{the defect was invisible
at $n=1$ and structural at $n=2$}, and it would have silently doubled the headline number.
