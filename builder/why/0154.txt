why    STEP 2: the cilium beats IN WATER, real MPM, and the honest result is that IT BARELY BEATS. The stand-in drag is gone -- the rod's nodes are entity: mpm_particle, so mpm_scatter and mpm_gather carry momentum between rod and water with NO new operator, and rod_elastic supplies all the stiffness because the rod's own Young's modulus is 1.0 against the water's bulk modulus of 1e5. That is an immersed boundary: MPM carries the momentum exchange, the springs carry the material. Water sampling is textbook, taken from config/si_material which is the water in this repo that works: 8.00 particles per grid cell, density the grid sees 987 to 1000 against a spec of 1000, CFL clean, both checked with plexus.generators.mpm_cfl BEFORE the run. WHAT FAILED: the rod's base swing collapsed from 60.3 deg in air to 1.0 deg in water, the tip from 31.8 to 0.06, and the water moved 0.001 um/s with neighbouring cells' velocities agreeing at cos = 0.000, which is noise and not a flow. The cause is the B-spline stencil: each rod node scatters over 27 cells and gathers back a velocity averaged with about 216 water particles, roughly 14 times its own mass, so a thin light filament is locked to the fluid and cannot move relative to it. A moment sweep confirms it and finds the ceiling -- 10 gives a 5.1 deg base, 100 gives 18.3 deg with the tip still dead at a ratio of 0.10, and 1000 saturates because mpm_gather caps velocity at 0.4*dx/dt. This is the SAME limitation that produced the 6 um paddle and the 25 x 10 um blade on the larva, now measured on a clean rig: MPM enforces no-slip too strongly over too wide a stencil for a sub-cell filament.
