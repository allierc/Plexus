why    152's ROD, 20 um (the right length for a Platynereis prototroch cilium), IN WATER, AT Re = 1. The physically right answer is out of reach and the arithmetic says why. Water's kinematic viscosity is 1e-6 m2/s; in a box declared 50 um across one world unit is 5e-5 m, so water is nu = 400 world^2/s. Every run so far used nu = eta/rho = 1e-6 -- water's value IN METRES inside a box labelled 50 um, off by 4e8 -- which is why they sat at Re = 9e5 instead of the 2.3e-3 a cilium lives at. They were metre-scale tanks wearing a 50 um label. Raising nu to 400 is therefore a CORRECTION and not a hack, and it still cannot be run: explicit viscous diffusion needs dt < dx^2/(6 nu) = 1.0e-07, 4,915x more substeps, 428 s per FRAME, 95 hours for 4 seconds of simulated time. No grid saves it either, because dt scales as dx^2 and the cell that would permit the current substep is 1.10 world units, wider than the box. Physically: viscous momentum crosses the box in L^2/nu = 2.5 ms against a 1000 ms beat, so the fluid reaches steady state 400 times per stroke -- that IS the quasi-static Stokes regime, and an explicit solver has to resolve the very diffusion the regime treats as instantaneous. So this run sits at Re = 1, the boundary rather than the destination: nu = 0.90, grid 64 -> 32 (dt scales as dx^2, buying 4x) with the water resampled to 8.00 particles a cell, 35 substeps a frame, and the moment at 1.6e5 because viscous drag here is some 200x the inertial drag it replaces. WHAT IT TESTS: whether the tipping collapses as Re crosses 1. If it does, steady streaming was the cause and the diagnosis holds; if it does not, the diagnosis is wrong and we should know before building on it. (after the run)
